# Indian Economy & Banking Basics

**In 30 seconds**
- NNP = GNP − Depreciation ⭐; National Income = NNP at factor cost.
- GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA); NDP = GDP − Depreciation.
- Revenue receipts neither create a liability nor reduce assets ⭐ (taxes, fees, dividends); capital receipts do one of the two.
- Repo rate = rate at which banks borrow from RBI by selling securities with a buy-back promise ⭐; reverse repo is the opposite.
- Budget = "Annual Financial Statement", Article 112; presented on 1 February since 2017.
- Maharashtra has the largest state economy (GSDP) ⭐, followed by Tamil Nadu.
- ICICI was set up in 1955 with World Bank support ⭐; NABARD 1982; SIDBI 1990; NHB 1988.
- GST started 1 July 2017 (101st Amendment); GST Council under Art 279A is chaired by the Union Finance Minister.

## 1. National income ⭐

| Term | Formula / meaning |
|---|---|
| GDP | Market value of final goods and services produced **within** the country in a year |
| GNP | GDP + NFIA (income of residents abroad − income of foreigners in India) |
| NDP | GDP − Depreciation |
| NNP ⭐ | GNP − Depreciation |
| Factor cost vs market price | Market price = Factor cost + Net indirect taxes (indirect taxes − subsidies) |
| National Income | NNP at factor cost |
| GVA at basic prices | GVA at factor cost + production taxes − production subsidies (used since 2015) |
| Nominal vs real | Nominal = current prices; real = constant (base-year) prices |
| GDP deflator | (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100 |
| Per-capita income | National income ÷ population |

**Measurement methods**: (1) Production / value-added, (2) Income (rent + wages + interest + profit), (3) Expenditure (C + I + G + X − M). India mainly uses production and income methods.
- Who measures: **National Statistics Office (NSO)**, MoSPI (CSO and NSSO merged in 2019).
- History: Dadabhai Naoroji first estimated it (1867–68, "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"); V.K.R.V. Rao (1931–32) first scientific estimate; National Income Committee 1949 under P.C. Mahalanobis.
- Base year: 2011–12 in the older series; a newer base (2022–23) was being introduced in 2026 — check the current-affairs capsule.

## 2. Sectors, growth and development

| Sector | Covers |
|---|---|
| Primary | Agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining |
| Secondary | Manufacturing, construction, electricity, gas, water supply |
| Tertiary (services) | Trade, transport, banking, IT, education, health — largest share of GVA |
| Organised | Registered, follows labour laws, regular jobs |
| Unorganised | Small, scattered units; most of India's workers |

- Agriculture's share of GVA is small but it employs the largest share of workers.
- **Growth** = rise in output (GDP); **development** = growth + better life (health, education, equality).
- **HDI** (UNDP, from 1990; Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen): life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling), GNI per capita (PPP). India was 130th of 193 in HDR 2025; newer ranking — check the capsule.
- Gross National Happiness: Bhutan.

## 3. Inflation, poverty, unemployment

| Inflation type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demand-pull | Too much money chasing too few goods |
| Cost-push | Rise in input costs (wages, oil) |
| Creeping / walking / galloping / hyper | Below 3% / 3–10% / double digit / out of control |
| Stagflation | High inflation + low growth + unemployment |
| Deflation / disinflation | Fall in prices / fall in the rate of inflation |
| Headline / core | All items / excluding food and fuel |

| Index | Published by |
|---|---|
| CPI (Rural, Urban, Combined) | NSO, MoSPI — used by RBI for its target since 2014 |
| WPI | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce and Industry) |
| CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL | Labour Bureau (Ministry of Labour) |

CPI base was being moved to 2024 in 2026 — check the capsule.

**Poverty committees**: Y.K. Alagh (1979, calorie norm); Lakdawala (1993); Tendulkar (2009, 21.9% poor in 2011–12); Rangarajan (2014, 29.5%). NITI Aayog uses the Multidimensional Poverty Index (health, education, standard of living); its 2024 paper said about 24.8 crore people left multidimensional poverty between 2013–14 and 2022–23.

**Unemployment types**: disguised (extra workers on a farm, zero marginal productivity), seasonal (agriculture), structural (skills mismatch), frictional (between jobs), cyclical (recession), educated. Data: **PLFS** by NSO (monthly estimates from 2025).

## 5. Budget ⭐

- Art 112: Annual Financial Statement (the word "budget" is not used in the Constitution). Art 113 demands for grants, 114 Appropriation Bill, 116 vote on account, 110 Money Bill (Finance Bill), 266 Consolidated Fund, 267 Contingency Fund.
- Presented on **1 February** since 2017; Railway Budget merged with it in 2017. First Budget of independent India: R.K. Shanmukham Chetty, 26 November 1947.
- **Economic Survey**: prepared by the Chief Economic Adviser, tabled a day before the Budget.

| Part | Revenue | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| Receipts | Tax (income tax, GST, customs) and non-tax (fees, fines, dividends, interest) — no liability, no asset reduction ⭐ | Borrowings, loan recovery, disinvestment — create liability or reduce assets |
| Expenditure | Salaries, interest, subsidies, pensions — creates no asset | Roads, buildings, machinery, loans to states — creates assets |

| Deficit | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue deficit | Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts |
| Effective revenue deficit | Revenue deficit − grants for creating capital assets |
| Fiscal deficit | Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) = total borrowing |
| Primary deficit | Fiscal deficit − Interest payments |

- FRBM Act, 2003 sets deficit targets.
- **Budget 2026–27** (presented by Nirmala Sitharaman on 1 February 2026): headline numbers (size, fiscal-deficit target, tax changes) — see the current-affairs capsule.

## 6. Taxes and GST

| Direct (burden cannot be shifted) | Indirect (burden shifted to buyer) |
|---|---|
| Income tax, corporation tax, securities transaction tax | GST, customs duty, excise on petroleum and liquor |

- Progressive tax: rate rises with income. Regressive: falls as income rises. Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026.
- **GST**: 101st Amendment (2016); launched **1 July 2017**; idea from Kelkar Task Force (2003). CGST + SGST on sales within a state; **IGST** on inter-state sales (Art 269A).
- Original slabs: 0, 5, 12, 18, 28% (+ cess). **GST 2.0** (from 22 September 2025, 56th Council meeting): main slabs **5% and 18%**, plus 40% on luxury and sin goods. Any later change — check the capsule.
- **GST Council** (Art 279A): Union Finance Minister (chair), Union MoS Finance, state Finance Ministers; decision by 3/4 weighted vote (Centre 1/3, states 2/3).
- Outside GST: alcohol for human consumption; petroleum crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas (for now).

## 7. Money and banking ⭐

**RBI**: recommended by Hilton Young Commission (1926); RBI Act 1934; started **1 April 1935** (Kolkata, moved to Mumbai 1937); nationalised **1 January 1949**. First Governor Osborne Smith; first Indian Governor C.D. Deshmukh. Current Governor: Sanjay Malhotra (since December 2024).

| RBI function | Note |
|---|---|
| Currency issue | All notes except ₹1 note; ₹1 note and coins issued by Government of India (Ministry of Finance) |
| Banker to government | Centre and states; manages public debt |
| Banker's bank and lender of last resort | Holds CRR; lends in crisis |
| Credit control | Monetary policy |
| Custodian of forex reserves | Manages the rupee exchange rate |
| Regulator | Banks, NBFCs, payment systems |

**Monetary policy tools ⭐**

| Tool | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Repo rate ⭐ | Banks borrow overnight from RBI by selling government securities with a repurchase agreement — the **policy rate** |
| Reverse repo | RBI borrows from banks against securities (kept fixed at 3.35% since 2022 — check the capsule) |
| SDF (Standing Deposit Facility, 2022) | Banks park surplus with RBI **without collateral**; floor of the corridor = repo − 0.25% |
| MSF (Marginal Standing Facility, 2011) | Emergency overnight borrowing using SLR securities; ceiling = repo + 0.25% |
| Bank rate | Long-term lending rate to banks without collateral; aligned with MSF |
| CRR | Share of deposits (NDTL) kept as **cash with RBI**; earns no interest |
| SLR | Share of NDTL kept by banks themselves in cash, gold, approved securities |
| OMO | RBI buys/sells government securities to change liquidity |
| Qualitative tools | Margin requirement, credit rationing, moral suasion, selective credit control |

- To fight inflation: raise repo/CRR/SLR or sell securities (OMO). To boost growth: cut them.
- Current repo, SDF, MSF, CRR and SLR values as of October 2026 — check the current-affairs capsule.
- **MPC** (RBI Act amended 2016; first met October 2016; Urjit Patel Committee idea): **6 members** — RBI Governor (chair, casting vote), Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, one RBI officer, and **3 external members** named by the Centre for 4 years. Quorum 4; meets at least 4 times a year (usually 6). Inflation target: CPI 4% ± 2% (fixed for 2021–26; renewal details — check the capsule).

**Money supply (RBI)**

| Measure | Content |
|---|---|
| M0 (reserve money) | Currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI |
| M1 (narrow money) | Currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI |
| M2 | M1 + post office savings deposits |
| M3 (broad money) | M1 + time deposits with banks |
| M4 | M3 + all post office deposits (except NSC) |

**Types of banks**: scheduled (listed in 2nd Schedule of RBI Act) and non-scheduled; public sector (12 after 2020 mergers), private, foreign; **Regional Rural Banks** (from 2 October 1975; owned Centre 50%, sponsor bank 35%, state 15%); cooperative banks; **small finance banks** and **payments banks** (Nachiket Mor Committee; payments banks take deposits up to ₹2 lakh, cannot lend). Bank nationalisation: 14 banks on 19 July 1969, 6 more in 1980. SBI formed 1 July 1955 from Imperial Bank.

## Exam traps
1. NNP = GNP − Depreciation, **not** GDP − Depreciation (that is NDP) ⭐.
2. Repo: banks **borrow from** RBI; reverse repo: RBI borrows from banks ⭐.
3. Revenue receipts create no liability and reduce no asset; borrowings are capital receipts ⭐.
4. Highest GSDP is Maharashtra, not UP (UP has the largest population and Assembly) ⭐.
5. ICICI is 1955; NABARD 1982; SIDBI 1990 — do not swap ⭐.
6. ₹1 notes are issued by the Government of India, not RBI.
7. WPI is published by DPIIT's Office of Economic Adviser; CPI by NSO.
8. Fiscal deficit equals total borrowing; primary deficit removes only interest payments.
