# General Science (Class 10 level)

**In 30 seconds**
- DSSSB 2023–24 asked: diseases (STD, vector-borne, malaria in RBCs), vitamins (amla is not a vitamin A source), optics (secondary rainbow), gravitation (Kepler), natural acids (citric acid in orange) and space science (Voyager 1).
- Physics: g = 9.8 m/s², escape velocity 11.2 km/s, speed of light 3 × 10⁸ m/s, sound in air about 343 m/s.
- Primary rainbow = 1 internal reflection; secondary rainbow = 2 (fainter, colours reversed). The sky is blue because of scattering.
- Lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. Power P = 1/f (metres), in dioptres. Myopia → concave lens; hypermetropia → convex lens.
- Natural acids: citric (lemon, orange), lactic (curd), acetic (vinegar), tartaric (tamarind), oxalic (tomato, spinach), formic (ant sting), ascorbic (amla).
- Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E, K. C → scurvy, D → rickets, B1 → beriberi, A → night blindness, K → bleeding.
- Malaria: Plasmodium (protozoan), spread by female Anopheles; it multiplies in the liver and then in RBCs. Amoebiasis spreads through dirty water, not sex.
- ISRO: Chandrayaan-3 landed near the Moon's south pole (23 Aug 2023); Aditya-L1 orbits L1 (Jan 2024); Gaganyaan is India's human spaceflight mission.

## PHYSICS

### 2. Motion, force, work

- Equations: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as.
- Newton's 1st law = inertia; 2nd: F = ma (momentum p = mv); 3rd: action–reaction (rocket, recoil of a gun).
- Work W = F × s (joule). KE = ½mv²; PE = mgh. Power = work/time. Energy is conserved, only changed in form.

### 3. Gravitation ⭐

- F = G m₁m₂ / r²; G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg² (universal); g = 9.8 m/s² (local).
- g is highest at the poles, lowest at the equator, zero at Earth's centre, and falls with height and depth. Moon's g ≈ 1/6 of Earth's: weight changes, mass does not.
- Escape velocity: Earth 11.2 km/s, Moon 2.4 km/s. Orbital velocity near Earth ≈ 7.9 km/s. Geostationary orbit ≈ 36,000 km, period 24 hours.
- **Kepler's laws** ⭐: orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus; a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times (fastest when nearest the Sun); T² ∝ r³. Newton explained them with gravitation.

### 4. Sound

- A mechanical, longitudinal wave; needs a medium (no sound in vacuum). Speed: air about 343 m/s (20 °C), water about 1,500 m/s, steel about 5,000 m/s. Faster in solids and in warm, humid air.
- Echo needs at least 17.2 m to the reflector (sound persists 0.1 s in the ear). Audible range 20 Hz–20 kHz; infrasound below (earthquakes, elephants); ultrasound above (bats, dolphins, SONAR, ultrasonography).
- Pitch depends on frequency; loudness on amplitude (decibel). Sonic boom: faster than sound (Mach > 1).

### 5. Light and optics ⭐

| Concept | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Speed | 3 × 10⁸ m/s; sunlight reaches Earth in about 8 min 20 s |
| Reflection | Angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Plane mirror: virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted |
| Mirrors | Concave: shaving, dentist, torch reflectors, solar furnace. Convex: rear-view mirrors (wide view). Formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f; f = R/2 |
| Refraction | Snell's law n = sin i / sin r. Water 1.33, glass 1.5, diamond 2.42. Apparent depth; twinkling of stars; Sun seen 2 min before actual sunrise |
| Lenses | Convex (converging), concave (diverging). 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; P = 1/f (m); convex power +, concave − |
| Total internal reflection | Light from denser to rarer medium beyond the critical angle: diamond sparkle, optical fibres, mirage |
| Dispersion | White light splits into VIBGYOR in a prism (Newton); violet bends most, red least |
| Rainbow ⭐ | Refraction + dispersion + internal reflection in raindrops; seen opposite the Sun. Primary: 1 internal reflection, red outside. Secondary: **2 reflections**, fainter, colours reversed |
| Scattering | Blue sky (short wavelengths scatter most); red sunrise and sunset; clouds white; danger signals red (least scattered); sky looks black in space. Tyndall effect. Raman effect (C.V. Raman, 1928) |

| Eye defect | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Myopia (short sight) | Image in front of retina; eyeball too long | Concave lens |
| Hypermetropia (long sight) | Image behind retina | Convex lens |
| Presbyopia | Old age; weak ciliary muscles | Bifocal lens |
| Astigmatism / cataract | Uneven cornea / cloudy lens | Cylindrical lens / surgery |

Near point of a normal eye 25 cm; far point infinity. Persistence of vision about 1/16 s. Retina: rods (dim light), cones (colour).

### 6. Electricity and magnetism

- I = Q/t (ampere); V = W/Q (volt); **Ohm's law V = IR**; R = ρL/A.
- Series: R = R₁ + R₂ (same current). Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ (same voltage); house wiring is parallel.
- Power P = VI = I²R = V²/R; heating H = I²Rt (Joule). Home supply in India: 220 V, 50 Hz AC.
- Fuse: low melting point (tin–lead), in series with the live wire. Heater element: nichrome. Bulb filament: tungsten. Wires: live (red/brown), neutral (black/blue), earth (green).
- Oersted: current makes a magnetic field. Faraday: electromagnetic induction. Motor = Fleming's left-hand rule; generator = right-hand rule. Transformers work only on AC. Electromagnets use soft iron. Iron, cobalt and nickel are ferromagnetic.

### 7. Heat

- °F = 9/5 °C + 32; −40 °C = −40 °F; body temperature 37 °C = 98.6 °F; absolute zero 0 K = −273.15 °C.
- Conduction (solids), convection (fluids; sea and land breezes), radiation (no medium; Sun's heat).
- Water has a high specific heat and is densest at 4 °C, so ice floats. Steam burns worse than boiling water (latent heat). Evaporation cools. Pressure cookers raise the boiling point; at high altitude water boils below 100 °C.

### 8. Electromagnetic spectrum

Increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength): **radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma**. All travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum. Uses: radio (broadcast), microwave (ovens, radar), infrared (TV remote, night vision), UV (sterilising, vitamin D; ozone blocks it), X-ray (bones; Röntgen 1895), gamma (cancer therapy).

## CHEMISTRY

### 9. Matter and atoms

- States: solid, liquid, gas, plasma (stars) and Bose–Einstein condensate (S.N. Bose). Sublimation: camphor, naphthalene, iodine, ammonium chloride, dry ice.
- Electron: J.J. Thomson (1897). Proton: Goldstein's canal rays, named by Rutherford. Nucleus: Rutherford's gold-foil experiment (1911). Neutron: Chadwick (1932). Bohr: fixed orbits; shell capacity 2n².
- Isotopes (same atomic number): protium, deuterium, tritium; C-14 (dating), U-235 (fuel), Co-60 (cancer), I-131 (thyroid). Isobars (same mass number): Ar-40 and Ca-40.
- **Periodic table:** Mendeleev (1869, by atomic mass); modern law by Moseley (atomic number); 18 groups, 7 periods, 118 elements. Across a period, size and metallic character fall; down a group, they rise. Fluorine is the most electronegative element. Liquids at room temperature: mercury (metal) and bromine (non-metal).
- **Bonding:** ionic (electron transfer; NaCl; high melting point; conducts when molten or dissolved); covalent (sharing; CH₄; low melting point).

### 11. Metals and non-metals

- Earth's crust: oxygen most abundant element; aluminium most abundant metal. Air: nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, argon 0.93%, CO₂ about 0.04%.
- Best conductor: silver, then copper. Lightest metal: lithium. Densest: osmium. Highest melting point: tungsten. Hardest natural substance: diamond. Graphite is a non-metal that conducts.
- Sodium and potassium are soft and kept in kerosene; white phosphorus in water.
- Ores: bauxite (Al), haematite (Fe), cinnabar (Hg), galena (Pb), zinc blende (Zn).
- Rust = hydrated iron oxide; galvanising = zinc coating. Alloys: brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni), solder (Pb + Sn), amalgam (with Hg). 24-carat gold is pure.

### 12. Carbon compounds

- Carbon shows catenation and tetravalency. Allotropes: diamond, graphite, fullerene (C-60), graphene.
- Common names: marsh gas = methane (main part of CNG and biogas); LPG = butane + propane (ethyl mercaptan added for smell); alcohol = ethanol; vinegar = 5–8% ethanoic acid; esters smell sweet.
- Soaps are sodium salts of fatty acids (saponification) and fail in hard water; detergents work in hard water. Vegetable ghee comes from hydrogenating oils with a nickel catalyst.
- Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, alkenes CₙH₂ₙ, alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂; a homologous series differs by CH₂.

## BIOLOGY

### 13. Cell and tissues

- Cell discovered by Robert Hooke (1665, cork); nucleus by Robert Brown (1831). Cell theory: Schleiden and Schwann; Virchow added "all cells arise from cells".
- Mitochondria: powerhouse (ATP, own DNA). Ribosomes: protein synthesis. Lysosomes: "suicide bags". Golgi: packaging. Plastids (chloroplasts) and cellulose walls only in plants. Mammalian RBCs have no nucleus.
- Plant tissues: meristem (growth), xylem (water; dead cells), phloem (food; living). Animal tissues: epithelial, connective (blood, bone; tendon joins muscle to bone, ligament joins bone to bone), muscular (striated, smooth, cardiac), nervous.

### 14. Life processes and human systems

- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (chlorophyll, sunlight, stomata). Anaerobic respiration: yeast → ethanol + CO₂; muscles → lactic acid (cramps).

### 17. Blood groups, genetics, reproduction

| Group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A | anti-B | — |
| B | B | anti-A | — |
| AB | A and B | none | Universal recipient |
| O | none | anti-A and anti-B | Universal donor |

ABO groups: Karl Landsteiner (1900–01). Rh factor (1940) is named after the rhesus monkey; an Rh− mother with an Rh+ baby risks erythroblastosis fetalis.

- **Mendel** (pea plant, 7 traits; father of genetics): monohybrid ratio 3:1 (genotype 1:2:1); dihybrid 9:3:3:1; laws of dominance, segregation and independent assortment. Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); the father's sperm (X or Y) decides the child's sex. DNA double helix: Watson and Crick (1953).
- **Reproduction:** asexual by fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra, yeast), fragmentation (Spirogyra), spores (Rhizopus) and vegetative parts (potato tuber, Bryophyllum leaf); sexual by fusion of gametes.

### 18. Ecology and environment

- Food chain: producers → consumers → decomposers; only 10% energy passes to the next level (Lindeman). Pesticides like DDT increase up the chain (biomagnification).
- India has 4 of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar). Conservation: in situ (national parks, biosphere reserves) and ex situ (zoos, seed banks).
- Pollution: CO blocks haemoglobin; SO₂ and NOₓ cause acid rain ("marble cancer" of the Taj Mahal); Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) was methyl isocyanate; Minamata disease = mercury; Itai-itai = cadmium; blue-baby syndrome = nitrates; fluorosis = excess fluoride.
- Ozone layer (stratosphere) absorbs UV; damaged by CFCs; Montreal Protocol 1987 (World Ozone Day 16 Sept); Kigali Amendment 2016 on HFCs.
- Greenhouse gases: CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour, CFCs/HFCs. Kyoto Protocol 1997; Paris Agreement 2015 (well below 2 °C, aim 1.5 °C). India's net-zero target: 2070.

## Exam traps

1. Amla is a source of vitamin C, not vitamin A.
2. Secondary rainbow = two internal reflections (not one, not three); its colours are reversed.
3. Kepler gave the laws of planetary motion; Newton gave the law of gravitation.
4. Tomato contains oxalic acid; orange and lemon contain citric acid; ant stings contain formic acid.
5. Amoebiasis is a water-borne protozoan disease, not an STD.
6. Myopia is corrected by a concave lens (not convex).
7. Universal donor is O; universal recipient is AB.
8. Voyager 1 (not New Horizons or Sputnik) was the first craft to cross the heliosphere.
