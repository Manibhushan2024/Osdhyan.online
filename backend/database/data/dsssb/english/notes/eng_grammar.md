# Grammar: Tenses, Articles, Prepositions, Agreement, Voice & Narration

**In 30 seconds**
- DSSSB rarely asks "name the tense". It hides grammar inside **error spotting, fill in the blank and sentence improvement** — so learn each rule as a pair: ✔ correct sentence, ✘ wrong sentence.
- **Prepositions are the most-asked grammar point** ⭐ (5 of 80 questions in 2023–24): *engage in*, *walk along the beach*, *pour from the flask into the cups*, *brim with tears*, *for thousands of years*.
- **Question tags** ⭐: positive statement → negative tag and the other way round; repeat the same auxiliary (*You can't hear..., can you?* / *She was wearing..., wasn't she?*).
- **Linking verbs take adjectives, action verbs take adverbs** ⭐: *He chewed slowly*, but *The rose smells sweet*.
- **Concession = though/although**, never *whether* ⭐ (*Though I do not agree with her, I respect her*).
- Tenses: *since/for* + action continuing now → present perfect (continuous); two past actions → the earlier one in past perfect; *if*-clause never takes *will*.
- Agreement: *each, every, either, neither, many a, the number of* → singular; *a number of, the police, cattle, people* → plural; with *neither…nor / either…or* the verb follows the nearer subject.
- Voice and narration were not asked in the four 2023–24 TGT CS papers, but they are on the syllabus and in other DSSSB papers — learn the two conversion tables below.

## 1. What DSSSB asked (2023–24, grammar points)

| Paper question (short) | Rule tested | ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| *engage on dust bathing* → error | **engage in** an activity | ⭐ |
| *walk through the beach* → error | walk **along/on** a beach; *through* = inside (forest, tunnel) | ⭐ |

| *eyes brim ___ tears* | brim **with** | ⭐ |

| *You can't hear the birds, ___?* | **can you** | ⭐ |
| *She was wearing a ball gown, ___?* | **wasn't she** | ⭐ |
| *Whether I do not agree...* → error | **Though/Although** for contrast | ⭐ |
| *He chewed ___* | adverb **slowly** after an action verb | ⭐ |
| *her being honesty* → error | **being + adjective** (*her being honest*) | ⭐ |

## 2. Tenses

### 2.1 The twelve tenses at a glance

| Tense | Form | Use / signal words | ✔ Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple present | V1 / V1+s | habits, facts, timetables; *always, every day, usually* | She **teaches** at a Delhi school. |
| Present continuous | is/am/are + V-ing | action now; *Look!, Listen!, now, at present* | Look! The children **are playing**. |
| Present perfect | has/have + V3 | past action, present result; *just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, recently* | I **have seen** this film three times. |
| Present perfect continuous | has/have been + V-ing | action began in past and is still going on; *since, for, how long* | She **has been living** in Delhi since 2015. |
| Simple past | V2 | finished action at a stated past time; *yesterday, ago, last year, in 2010* | He **left** two hours ago. |
| Past continuous | was/were + V-ing | action in progress at a past moment | I **was reading** when she called. |
| Past perfect | had + V3 | the **earlier** of two past actions | The train **had left** before we reached. |
| Past perfect continuous | had been + V-ing | past action going on for a time before another | He **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus came. |
| Simple future | will/shall + V1 | future fact, decision | I **will call** you tomorrow. |
| Future continuous | will be + V-ing | in progress at a future moment; *this time tomorrow* | This time next week we **will be lying** on the beach. |
| Future perfect | will have + V3 | complete **by** a future time | I **will have finished** the report by evening. |
| Future perfect continuous | will have been + V-ing | duration up to a future time | By June she **will have been teaching** for ten years. |

### 2.2 Tense rules that become error-spotting questions

| Rule | ✔ Correct | ✘ Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| *Since* (point of time) / *for* (period) + action continuing now → present perfect (continuous) | I **have known** him **since** 2010. | I *know* him since 2010. / I *am knowing* him... |
| Stative verbs (know, believe, understand, love, hate, own, belong, seem, contain, prefer) are not used in continuous forms | He **knows** the answer. | He *is knowing* the answer. |
| A definite past time takes the simple past, not present perfect | I **met** him yesterday. | I *have met* him yesterday. |
| *Did/didn't/does/do* + base verb | She **didn't know** the answer. | She *didn't knew*... |
| Two past actions: earlier = past perfect | The patient **had died** before the doctor came. | The patient *died* before the doctor *had come*. |
| Universal truth stays in simple present, even after a past reporting verb | He said that the earth **moves** round the sun. | ...the earth *moved*... |
| *If*-clause of a real future condition takes simple present | **If it rains**, we will stay home. | If it *will rain*, ... |
| Unreal present: *If I were* (all persons) + would | If I **were** you, I would accept. | If I *was*/ *am* you, I *will* accept. |
| Unreal past (3rd conditional): *If + had + V3*, *would have + V3* | If you **had asked**, I would have helped. | If you *would have asked*... |
| *It is time / It is high time* + simple past | It is high time we **left**. | It is high time we *leave*. |
| *Wish* (present unreal) + past / *were* | I wish I **were** taller. | I wish I *am* taller. |
| *Lest* + should + V1 | Walk carefully lest you **should fall**. | ...lest you *will fall* / *should not fall*. |
| *Hardly/Scarcely … when*; *No sooner … than* + past perfect with inversion ⭐ | **No sooner had** he left **than** it rained. **Hardly had** I entered **when** the phone rang. | No sooner *he had left*... / Hardly had I entered *than*... |
| *As if / as though* (unreal) + past / *were* | He talks as if he **were** the boss. | ...as if he *is* the boss. |
| Two actions joined by *and* in the same time frame keep the same tense | She **opened** the door and **saw** a snake. | She opened the door and *sees*... |

**Conditionals in one table**

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | simple present | simple present | always true: *If you heat ice, it melts.* |
| First | simple present | will + V1 | likely future: *If he comes, I will tell him.* |
| Second | simple past / were | would + V1 | unreal now: *If I were rich, I would travel.* |
| Third | had + V3 | would have + V3 | unreal past: *If she had studied, she would have passed.* |

Inverted forms are also correct: *Had I known* = *If I had known*; *Were I you* = *If I were you*; *Should you need help* = *If you should need help*.

## 3. Articles

| Rule | ✔ Example | ✘ Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| *A/an* depends on the **sound**, not the letter | **an** hour, **an** honest man, **an** MBA, **an** MLA, **an** X-ray | *a* hour, *a* MBA |
| Consonant sound /yu/ or /w/ takes *a* | **a** university, **a** European, **a** one-day match, **a** useful book | *an* university, *an* European |
| *The* with rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, deserts, groups of islands | **the** Ganga, **the** Indian Ocean, **the** Himalayas, **the** Thar, **the** Andamans | *Ganga* is holy (in exam usage) |
| No article with single mountain peaks, lakes, countries (singular names), cities | Mount Everest, Lake Wular, India, Delhi | *the* Mount Everest, *the* India |
| *The* with plural or "union" country names | **the** USA, **the** UK, **the** Netherlands | |
| *The* with unique things, superlatives, ordinals, musical instruments | **the** sun, **the** best, **the** first, play **the** guitar | play *guitar* (exam usage) |
| *The* with holy books, newspapers, famous buildings | **the** Gita, **the** Hindu, **the** Taj Mahal | |
| No article with games, meals, languages, subjects, diseases (most) | play cricket, have lunch, speak Hindi, study physics, suffer from malaria | play *the* cricket, speak *the* Hindi |
| No article when school, church, hospital, prison, bed are used for their **main purpose** | He goes **to school**. She is **in hospital**. | He goes to *the* school (as a student). |
| *The* when the place is visited for another purpose | Her mother went **to the school** to meet the teacher. | |
| *What a / such a / quite a / many a* + singular countable noun | **What a** pity! **Many a** student has failed. | What *pity*! |
| *The + adjective* = a class of people (plural verb) | **The rich are** not always happy. | *Rich* are... |
| *The* before a proper noun used as a common noun | Kalidas is **the** Shakespeare of India. | |
| Second mention of a noun takes *the* | I saw **a** dog. **The** dog was hungry. | |

## 4. Prepositions ⭐ (the most-asked grammar point)

### 4.1 Time and place

| Preposition | Time | Place |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | exact time, festivals, night: *at 6 o'clock, at Diwali, at night, at noon* | a point/small place: *at the gate, at the bus stop* |
| **on** | days and dates: *on Monday, on 15 August, on my birthday* | a surface or line: *on the table, on the wall, on the road* |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, parts of day, after a period: *in May, in 2026, in summer, in the morning, in two days* | inside an area: *in the room, in Delhi, in India* |
| **since** | point of time (with perfect tense): *since Monday, since 2015* | — |
| **for** | period of time: *for two hours, for thousands of years* ⭐ | — |
| **by** | not later than: *by 5 p.m., by tomorrow* | beside: *by the window* |
| **within** | before the end of a period: *within a week* | inside limits: *within the campus* |
| **between / among** | *between 2 and 3 p.m.* | **between** two; **among** more than two: *divide among the three children* |
| **along / across / through** | — | **along** = following a line (*walk along the beach* ⭐, *along the river*); **across** = from one side to the other (*swim across the river*); **through** = inside from end to end (*through the forest/tunnel*) |
| **into / in** | — | **into** = movement inwards (*jump into the river, pour into the cups* ⭐); **in** = position (*swimming in the river*) |
| **from … into / to** | *from Monday to Friday* | source → destination: *pour coffee from the flask into the cups* ⭐ |
| **beside / besides** | — | **beside** = next to; **besides** = in addition to |

### 4.2 Fixed prepositions (learn as one word)

| Word + preposition | ✔ Example | ✘ Common wrong form |
|---|---|---|
| **engage in** (an activity) ⭐ | Sparrows **engage in** dust bathing. | engage *on* |
| **brim with** ⭐ | Her eyes **brimmed with** tears. | brim *of/by* |
| **benefit from** ⭐ | We have **benefited from** the sea. | benefit *of* |
| **take to** (the streets = protest) ⭐ | Residents **took to** the streets. | took *on* the streets |
| good **at**, weak **in** | She is **good at** maths. | good *in* maths |
| congratulate **on** | congratulated him **on** his success | *for* his success |
| married **to** | She is **married to** a doctor. | married *with* |
| suffer **from** | **suffering from** fever | suffering *with/by* |
| annoyed **with** (person), **at/about** (thing) | annoyed **with** the students | annoyed *on* |
| accuse **of**, charge **with** | **accused of** theft; **charged with** murder | accused *for* theft |
| senior/junior/superior/inferior/prior **to**; prefer A **to** B | senior **to** me; prefer tea **to** coffee | senior *than* me |
| beware **of** | **Beware of** dogs. | beware *from* |
| absent **from** | **absent from** class | absent *in* |
| comply **with**, agree **with** (person), agree **to** (proposal) | **comply with** the rules | comply *to* |
| abstain **from**, refrain **from**, prevent **from** | **refrain from** smoking | refrain *to* smoke |
| aim **at**, arrive **at** (small place) / **in** (city, country) | **arrived in** Delhi | arrived *to* Delhi |
| afraid **of**, fond **of**, proud **of**, jealous **of**, aware **of** | **fond of** music | fond *for* music |
| capable **of**, confident **of**, guilty **of** | **capable of** doing it | capable *to* do |
| interested **in**, rich **in**, deficient **in**, expert **in/at** | **interested in** art | interested *for* |
| angry **with** (person), **at** (thing) | angry **with** him | angry *on* him |
| depend/rely **on**, insist **on**, comment **on**, lay stress **on** | **insist on** going | insist *to* go |
| listen **to**, reply **to**, object **to**, belong **to**, according **to** | **listen to** music | listen *music* |
| discuss, enter, reach, attack, resemble, request, order, approach — **no preposition** | We **discussed the matter**. | discussed *about* the matter |
| despite — **no preposition**; in spite **of** | **Despite** the rain / **In spite of** the rain | *despite of* |
| die **of** (disease), die **from** (wound, cause), die **by** (violence) | died **of** cholera | died *with* cholera |
| divide **into** (parts), divide **among/between** (people) | **divided into** three parts | divided *in* three parts |
| independent **of**, dependent **on** | **independent of** others | independent *from* |
| sympathy **for**, sympathise **with** | **sympathise with** the poor | sympathise *for* |
| full **of**, filled **with** | **full of** water, **filled with** water | full *with* |
| at the age **of**, **on** foot, **by** bus/train/car, **in** a car | went **on** foot | went *by* foot |

## 5. Subject–verb agreement

| Rule | ✔ Correct | ✘ Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| *Each, every, either, neither, everyone, somebody, nobody* → singular | **Each** of the boys **was** given a prize. | Each of the boys *were*... |
| *Many a* + singular noun + singular verb | **Many a student has** failed. | Many a student *have* failed. |
| *The number of* → singular; *a number of* → plural | **The number of** applicants **is** rising. **A number of** students **were** absent. | The number of applicants *are*... |
| *Neither…nor / either…or / not only…but also* → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | Neither the manager nor **the employees were** present. | ...nor the employees *was*... |
| *As well as, along with, together with, with, besides, like, in addition to* → verb agrees with the **first** subject | **The Principal**, along with the teachers, **is** planning a party. | ...*are* planning... |
| Two subjects joined by *and* → plural; but one idea or one person → singular | Ram and Shyam **are** friends. **Bread and butter is** my breakfast. **The poet and painter is** here (one person). | |
| *-ics* subjects (maths, physics, economics, politics as a subject), news, measles → singular | **Mathematics is** my favourite subject. | Mathematics *are*... |
| Uncountable nouns: furniture, luggage, baggage, advice, information, scenery, machinery, equipment, poetry → singular, no plural form | **The furniture is** made of teak. **The scenery is** charming. | furniture *are*; *advices*; *sceneries* |
| Always-plural nouns: police, cattle, people, poultry, clergy, gentry → plural verb | **The police are** investigating. | The police *is*... |
| Nouns with plural form and plural verb: scissors, trousers, spectacles, thanks, goods, riches, premises | **My spectacles are** broken. | |
| Distance, money, time, weight as one amount → singular | **Ten kilometres is** a long walk. | Ten kilometres *are*... |
| Collective noun: one unit → singular; members acting separately → plural | **The jury has** given its verdict. **The jury were** divided in their opinions. | |
| *One of the + plural noun* → singular verb; *one of the + plural noun + who/that* → plural verb | **One of the boys is** absent. Rahul is one of the boys **who have** won medals. | One of the boys *are*... / ...who *has* won |
| *The + adjective* (the rich, the poor) → plural | **The poor are** honest. | |
| *More than one* + singular noun → singular | **More than one student was** absent. | |
| *A lot of / plenty of / most of / some of / all of* → depends on the noun after *of* | **Most of the water is** wasted. **Most of the students are** here. | |
| Title of a book or a country name in plural form → singular | **The United States is** big. *Gulliver's Travels* **is** a classic. | |

## 6. Active and passive voice

**Rule**: object of the active → subject of the passive; verb → *be* (in the same tense) + V3; subject → *by* + object form. Only **transitive** verbs have a passive.

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Present perfect | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Simple past | He **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past continuous | He **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Past perfect | He **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Simple future | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Future perfect | He **will have written** a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by him. |
| Modals | He **can/must/should write** a letter. | A letter **can/must/should be written** by him. |

The perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous have no passive in exam usage.

| Special case | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Imperative (order) | Do it at once. | **Let it be done** at once. / You are ordered to do it at once. |
| Imperative (request) | Please help me. | **You are requested to** help me. |
| *Who* question | Who wrote this letter? | **By whom was** this letter written? |
| *Whom* question | Whom did you invite? | **Who was invited** by you? |
| Yes/No question | Did he finish the work? | **Was** the work **finished** by him? |
| *Wh*- question | Why did she beat the dog? | Why **was** the dog **beaten** by her? |
| Vague agent (someone, people, they) → drop it | Someone has stolen my bicycle. | My bicycle **has been stolen**. |
| Two objects | He gave me a book. | **I was given** a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. |
| Preposition with the verb | They laughed at him. | He **was laughed at** by them. |
| Verbs with *with/at/to* instead of *by* | The news surprised me. Everyone knows him. | I **was surprised at** the news. He **is known to** everyone. |
| *Let* sentence | Let me do it. | **Let it be done** by me. |
| Infinitive | It is time to close the shop. | It is time **for the shop to be closed**. |

✘ Common errors: *A letter is being wrote*; *The thief was arrest by police*; *By whom this letter was written?* (needs question order); passive of intransitive verbs (*He was died* ✘).

## 7. Direct and indirect speech (narration)

**Step 1 — reporting verb**: *said* → *said* (statement), *said to* → *told*; question → *asked/enquired*; order → *ordered/commanded*; request (*please*) → *requested*; advice → *advised*; *Let's* → *proposed/suggested that … should*; exclamation → *exclaimed with joy/sorrow*.

**Step 2 — tense** (only if the reporting verb is in the past):

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| simple present (*am/is/are, write*) | simple past (*was/were, wrote*) |
| present continuous | past continuous |
| present perfect | past perfect |
| simple past | past perfect (may stay past for a clear time) |
| will / shall / can / may | would / should / could / might |
| must (obligation) | had to |

**No change**: universal truths, habitual facts and historical facts (*The teacher said that the earth **moves** round the sun*); reporting verb in present or future (*He says that he **is** ill*); *would, should, could, might, ought to, used to*.

**Step 3 — pronouns**: first person → like the subject of the reporting verb; second person → like the object of the reporting verb; third person — no change.

**Step 4 — time and place words**

| Direct | Indirect | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|---|
| now | then | today | that day |
| here | there | tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| this / these | that / those | yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| ago | before | last night | the previous night |
| thus | so | next week | the following week |

| Sentence type | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | He said, "I am happy." | He said **that he was** happy. |
| Statement with object | She said to me, "I will come tomorrow." | She **told me that she would** come **the next day**. |
| Wh-question | She said to me, "Where do you live?" | She **asked me where I lived**. (statement order, no *that*, no ?) |
| Yes/no question | He said to me, "Are you ready?" | He **asked me if/whether I was** ready. |
| Order | The teacher said, "Sit down." | The teacher **ordered** the boys **to sit** down. |
| Negative order | He said, "Don't shout." | He **told** them **not to shout**. |
| Request | He said to her, "Please help me." | He **requested her to help** him. |
| *Let's* (suggestion) | The captain said, "Let us play carefully." | The captain **proposed that they should** play carefully. |
| *Let* (permission) | He said, "Let me go." | He **requested that he might be allowed** to go. |
| Exclamation | "Alas! I have failed," said the boy. | The boy **exclaimed sorrowfully that he had failed**. |
| Wish | She said, "May God bless you." | She **prayed that God might bless** me/him. |

✘ Common errors: *She asked me where did I live*; *He told that...* (told needs an object); *He said me...*; keeping "?" in the indirect question.

## 8. Question tags ⭐, conjunctions ⭐, adjectives vs adverbs ⭐

**Question tags**

| Rule | ✔ Example |
|---|---|
| Positive statement → negative tag; negative → positive | She **was** wearing a gown, **wasn't she?** You **can't** hear, **can you?** ⭐ |
| Repeat the same auxiliary; with no auxiliary use do/does/did | He **plays** well, **doesn't he?** They **went** home, **didn't they?** |
| *I am* → **aren't I** | I am right, **aren't I?** |
| *Let's* → **shall we**; *Let me/him* → **will you** | Let's go, **shall we?** Let me help, **will you?** |
| Imperative → **will/won't you** | Shut the door, **will you?** |
| Never, hardly, scarcely, seldom, nobody, nothing, few make the sentence negative → positive tag | He **hardly** works, **does he?** **Nobody** came, **did they?** |
| Everyone/someone/nobody → **they**; everything/nothing → **it**; *this/that* → **it** | **Everything** is fine, **isn't it?** |
| *There is* → *isn't there* | There is a bank nearby, **isn't there?** |
| Tag pronoun is never a noun | Ravi is late, **isn't he?** (✘ *isn't Ravi?*) |

**Conjunctions**

| Pair / rule | ✔ Correct | ✘ Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast: **though/although … (no *but*)** ⭐ | **Though** he was tired, he continued. | Though he was tired, *but* he continued. / *Whether* I do not agree... |
| *Whether … or*; *either … or*; *neither … nor*; *both … and*; *not only … but also* | **Neither** Ravi **nor** Mohan came. | Neither Ravi *or* Mohan came. |
| *No sooner … than*; *hardly/scarcely … when*; *rather … than*; *such … that/as*; *so … that* | He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk. | ...so weak *as* he cannot walk |
| *Unless* = if not (no *not* after it) | **Unless** you work hard, you will fail. | Unless you *do not* work hard... |
| *Until* = up to a time (no *not* after it) | Wait **until** I return. | Wait until I *do not* return. |
| *Lest … should* | Run fast **lest** you **should** miss the bus. | lest you *should not* miss |
| *Because* (reason) vs *so that* (purpose) vs *although* (contrast) | He stayed home **because** he was ill. | |
| *Between … and* (not *or/to*) | **between** Delhi **and** Agra | between Delhi *to* Agra |

**Adjective or adverb?**

| Rule | ✔ | ✘ |
|---|---|---|
| Action verb → adverb ⭐ | He chewed **slowly**. She sings **beautifully**. | He chewed *slow*. |
| Linking verb (be, seem, look, feel, smell, taste, sound, appear, become, grow, remain) → adjective | The rose smells **sweet**. She looks **happy**. | smells *sweetly*; looks *happily* |
| After *being*, use an adjective ⭐ | I appreciate her **being honest**. | her being *honesty* |
| *Very* + positive adjective; *much* + comparative or past participle | **very** beautiful; **much** better; **much** admired | *very much beautiful*; *very better* |
| No double comparative/superlative | She is **taller** than her sister. | *more taller* |
| *Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer* + **to** | He is senior **to** me. | senior *than* me |
| Comparison with *any other* + singular noun | He is taller than **any other boy**. | ...than *any boy* (when he is in the same class) |
| *Elder/eldest* for family; *older/oldest* for age in general | my **elder** brother | my *older* brother is *elder* than... |
| *Fewer* (countable) / *less* (uncountable) | **fewer** students, **less** water | *less* students |
| *Hard* = with effort; *hardly* = scarcely | He works **hard**. He **hardly** works (= almost not). | |
| *Late* = not early; *lately* = recently | He came **late**. I haven't seen him **lately**. | |

**Pronouns (case)**

| Rule | ✔ | ✘ |
|---|---|---|
| Object form after a preposition | Between you and **me** | between you and *I* |
| Subject form after *than/as* when a verb is understood | He is taller than **I** (am). | taller than *me* (formal exam answer: I) |
| Order of personal pronouns: 2nd, 3rd, 1st (231) for good things; 1st first for blame | **You, he and I** will go. **I, you and he** are guilty. | |
| *One* is followed by *one's* | **One** must do **one's** duty. | One must do *his* duty |
| *Who* (subject) / *whom* (object) | The man **who** called... The man **whom** we met... | |

## Exam traps
1. **Despite** never takes *of*; **in spite of** always does. ✘ *despite of*.
2. **Hardly/Scarcely** pair with **when**; only **No sooner** pairs with **than** — and all three take the past perfect with inversion ⭐ (*Hardly had I...*).
3. **Prefer A to B**, **senior to**, **superior to** — never *than*.
4. **Each / every / either / neither / many a / the number of** → singular verb; **a number of / the police / cattle / people** → plural.
5. **Furniture, luggage, advice, information, scenery, equipment** have no plural and take a singular verb (*a piece of advice*, ✘ *advices*).
6. **Stative verbs** (know, believe, understand, own, love) do not take *-ing* (✘ *I am knowing him*).
7. **If-clause never takes will** (✘ *If he will come*); unreal present uses **were** for all persons.
8. In narration, **universal truths keep the present tense**, and **reported questions lose the question order** (✘ *asked me where did I live*).

## One-liners for revision
1. *Since* + point of time; *for* + period of time.
2. *Since 2015* + action still going on → *has been living*.
3. Earlier of two past actions → past perfect.
4. *By tomorrow evening* → future perfect (*will have finished*).
5. *This time next week* → future continuous (*will be lying*).
6. *Look! / Listen!* → present continuous.
7. Universal truth stays in simple present.
8. *If I were you...* — *were* for all persons.
9. Third conditional: *If + had + V3, would have + V3*.
10. *It is high time we left* — past form after *it is time*.
11. *Lest* is followed by *should*, never by *not*.
12. *An* hour, *an* honest man, *an* MBA — vowel sound.
13. *A* university, *a* European, *a* one-rupee coin — consonant sound.
14. *The* Ganga, *the* Himalayas, *the* Indian Ocean; no article for Mount Everest.
15. Play *the* guitar, but play cricket.
16. Go to school / to bed / to hospital — no article when used for the main purpose.
17. *Engage in* an activity ⭐.
18. Walk *along* a beach, *through* a forest ⭐.
19. Pour *from* the flask *into* the cups ⭐.
20. Eyes *brim with* tears ⭐.
21. *For* thousands of years — duration ⭐.
22. *Took to the streets* = protested ⭐.
23. Congratulate *on*; accuse *of*; charge *with*.
24. Married *to*; suffer *from*; absent *from*.
25. Discuss, enter, reach, resemble — no preposition.
26. Divide *among* three or more; *between* two.
27. *Neither…nor*: verb follows the nearer subject.
28. *As well as / along with*: verb follows the first subject.
29. *Bread and butter is* — one idea, singular.
30. *Mathematics is*; *the police are*; *the furniture is*.
31. *One of the boys who have won* — plural after *who*.
32. Present perfect passive: *has/have been + V3*.
33. Present continuous passive: *is/am/are being + V3*.
34. Imperative passive: *Let it be done*.
35. *Who wrote it?* → *By whom was it written?*
36. *Said to* → *told*; question → *asked*; request → *requested*.
37. *Tomorrow* → *the next day*; *yesterday* → *the previous day*.
38. *Let's go* → tag *shall we?* ⭐
39. *I am right* → tag *aren't I?*
40. Action verb + adverb (*chewed slowly*); linking verb + adjective (*smells sweet*) ⭐.

## Quick self-check
1. *The sparrows engage ___ dust bathing.* — **in.** ⭐
2. *She ___ in Delhi since 2015.* — **has been living.**
3. *Neither the teacher nor the students ___ aware.* — **were** (nearer subject *students*).
4. *Many a student ___ failed.* — **has.**
5. *Hardly had I entered ___ the phone rang.* — **when.**
6. Passive of *Someone has stolen my bicycle.* — **My bicycle has been stolen.**
7. Indirect speech of *She said to me, "Where do you live?"* — **She asked me where I lived.**
8. *You can't hear the birds from outside, ___?* — **can you.** ⭐
9. *He took another bite and chewed ___ (slow/slowly).* — **slowly.** ⭐
10. Correct the error: *Whether I do not agree with her, I respect her.* — **Though/Although I do not agree...** ⭐
