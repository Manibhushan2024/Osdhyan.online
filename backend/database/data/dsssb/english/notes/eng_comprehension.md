# Reading Comprehension, Cloze Test & Para Jumbles

**In 30 seconds**
- **Every DSSSB paper has one passage with 5 questions** ⭐ — 20 of the 80 English questions in 2023–24. Passages are 250–400 words: a personal travel story, a feature on disappearing bicycle mechanics, an essay on unfair workplace competition, and a piece on India's population explosion.
- The 5 questions mix **title / main idea, tone, purpose, type of passage, factual detail, NOT-mentioned, inference, word meaning and antonym from the passage** ⭐.
- **Para jumbles** ⭐ came 7 times (about 2 per paper) — four sentences labelled 1–4, A–D or P–S; plus one **sentence-part rearrangement** ⭐.
- **Cloze test** was not asked in the four TGT CS papers but is on the syllabus and in other DSSSB papers; it tests collocations, prepositions, connectors and tone.
- Method for RC: **read the questions first, then the passage once, then answer from the text — not from your own opinion**.
- Method for para jumbles: **find the opener** (introduces the topic, no pronoun or connector), **lock the pairs** (pronoun → its noun, *however*, *as a result*, *the other*), **find the closer**, then match with options.
- Answer the passage last if you are slow, but never skip it: its 5 marks are the easiest in Section A once you use the method.

## 2. Reading comprehension — the method

| Step | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the **5 questions** (not the options). Mark the key words: *NOT, title, tone, infer, closest in meaning*. | 30 s |
| 2 | Read the passage **once**, fast but fully. Note in your head: topic, the writer's view, the turn words (*however, but, yet*). | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Answer **fact and word-meaning** questions first — go back to the exact line. | 1 min |
| 4 | Answer **title / main idea / tone / purpose / type** — think of the whole passage, not one paragraph. | 1 min |
| 5 | Answer **inference / NOT** questions by testing each option against the text. | 1 min |

A 5-question passage should take **6–7 minutes**. The 2023–24 papers allowed 120 minutes for 200 questions; the passage is worth that time because each answer is in the text.

### 2.1 Question types and how to answer them

| Type | What it asks | How to answer | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main idea / central theme** ⭐ | the point of the whole passage | Combine the first and last paragraphs; the answer covers the **whole** passage | an option that covers only one paragraph (*Great Wall of China*) |
| **Title** ⭐ | best heading | Short, covers all main parts (*Travel and Language* = travel + learning languages) | too broad (*The Beautiful World*) or too narrow |
| **Tone / attitude** ⭐ | writer's feeling | Look at adjectives and verbs: emotional, critical, neutral? | choosing a tone that is too strong (*sarcastic, angry*) |
| **Purpose of a line / device** ⭐ | why the writer used an anecdote or opening | Ask what comes right after it — the device introduces or proves that point | *No particular purpose* is almost never right |
| **Type of passage** ⭐ | narrative / descriptive / expository / persuasive / argumentative | See section 2.2 | confusing expository (explains) with argumentative (takes sides) |
| **Factual** ⭐ | a detail stated in the passage | Find the sentence; match the meaning, not just the words | options that mix two details (*Eiffel Tower at thirteen*) |
| **NOT mentioned / EXCEPT** ⭐ | the odd one out | Tick each option that **is** in the text; the leftover is the answer | an option that sounds true in real life but is not in the passage |
| **Inference** ⭐ | what follows from the text | Must be supported by a line, but not stated word for word | extreme words: *always, never, only, completely* |
| **Author would agree / disagree** ⭐ | the writer's view | Find the writer's own opinion lines (*I think, should, must*) | options that match the view of a person the writer criticises |
| **Word meaning in context** ⭐ | synonym of a word in the passage | Replace the word with each option in **that sentence** | the dictionary meaning that does not fit the context |
| **Antonym from the passage** ⭐ | find the opposite of a given word in the passage | The answer is a word that **appears in the passage** (*unique, prevailing*) | options that are synonyms of the given word (*lost, obsolete, defunct* for *extinct*) |

### 2.2 Types of passage and tone words

| Type | Purpose | Signs | Example from papers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative | tells a story/events in order | *When I was…, then, at five, at thirteen* | travel story ⭐ |
| Descriptive | paints a picture of a place, person, thing | many adjectives, senses | (travel story also descriptive) ⭐ |
| **Expository** | explains or informs about a topic: causes, effects, remedies | facts, explanations, neutral voice | workplace competition ⭐ |
| Persuasive | tries to make the reader act or believe | *we must, should, let us* | |
| Argumentative | takes a side and answers the other side with reasons | *critics say … but*, evidence on both sides | |

| Positive tones | Neutral tones | Negative tones |
|---|---|---|
| optimistic, appreciative, enthusiastic, laudatory, hopeful, humorous | objective, informative, analytical, narrative, descriptive, advisory, didactic (teaching) | critical, sarcastic, cynical, pessimistic, satirical, indignant, nostalgic (longing for the past), melancholic |

### 2.3 Rules for choosing between two close options
1. **Stay inside the passage.** If an option is true in real life but not in the text, it is wrong.
2. **Avoid extremes.** *Always, never, all, only, completely* are usually wrong in inference questions.
3. **Prefer the moderate, complete option** for title and main idea; reject options that cover one detail.
4. **Match the writer's voice.** A writer who explains facts is *informative*, not *angry*.
5. **Read one sentence before and after** the line a question points to — the answer often depends on them.

### 2.4 Question stems decoded

| Stem in the paper | What it really asks | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| *Which of the following is the most appropriate title?* | the whole passage in a few words | first and last paragraphs |
| *What is the tone of the passage?* | the writer's attitude | adjectives, verbs, any *I feel / we must* |
| *Why has the writer started with…?* | function of an anecdote or opening | the line right after the anecdote |
| *Which is NOT a consequence of…?* | the odd one out among listed effects | the paragraph that lists effects |
| *The writer would NOT agree with…* | the opposite of the writer's view | lines with *should, must, I think* |
| *What does the phrase … mean?* | figurative meaning in context | the phrase's sentence and the one before |
| *Which is the type of the passage?* | narrative / descriptive / expository / persuasive | the passage's overall purpose |
| *Select the word from the passage opposite to…* | a word printed in the passage | scan for the opposite idea |

### 2.5 A worked example

> *Most of us check our phones more than a hundred times a day, yet few of us can say what we gained from it. Studies show that constant notifications break attention into small pieces; a student who is interrupted every few minutes takes far longer to finish the same homework. This does not mean phones are harmful in themselves. Used with a plan — fixed times for messages, no phone at the study table — they are powerful tools for learning. The problem is not the device but the habit.*

| Question | Reasoning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Main idea? | Problem (constant checking) → evidence → not the phone but the habit | Uncontrolled phone **habits**, not phones themselves, harm attention |
| Tone? | Explains with a study, then advises | **Informative and advisory** |
| Word closest to *constant*? | *constant notifications* = coming again and again | **continual** |
| Which is NOT suggested? | Fixed times ✔, no phone at the study table ✔, *give up phones completely* ✘ | Giving up phones completely |
| Inference? | Interrupted student takes longer → | Fewer interruptions help students finish work faster |

## 3. Cloze test

A passage with 5 numbered blanks; each blank has 4 options. Every blank tests one of five things.

| What the blank tests | How to decide | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Collocation** | Which word naturally goes with its neighbour? | *take* responsibility; *suffer from* water shortage; *heavy* rain |
| **Grammar** | Tense, agreement, verb form, article | *the time young people **spend** on books* (plural subject *people*) |
| **Preposition** | Fixed pair | *live **in** fear of*; *well **before** the exam* |
| **Logic / meaning** | Does the word fit the cause–effect of the passage? | the **excessive** use of groundwater is the *reason* for shortage |
| **Tone / connector** | Does the word continue or reverse the idea? | *seldom **miss** their goals* — *seldom* is negative, so the verb must describe failure |

**Method**
1. Read the **whole passage first** with the blanks; get the topic and the writer's attitude (positive/negative).
2. For each blank, read the **full sentence** and the sentence before it.
3. Remove options that are the **wrong part of speech** or break a fixed collocation.
4. Of the remaining, choose the one that keeps the **logic and tone** of the passage.
5. Re-read the completed sentence once.

**Connectors you must know**

| Purpose | Connectors |
|---|---|
| Add | and, moreover, furthermore, besides, in addition, also |
| Contrast | but, however, yet, although, though, nevertheless, on the other hand, whereas |
| Cause | because, since, as, due to, owing to |
| Result | so, therefore, thus, hence, consequently, as a result |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, in case |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as |
| Sequence | first, then, next, finally, meanwhile, afterwards |
| Conclusion | in short, in conclusion, to sum up, on the whole |

**A worked cloze**

> *Water is (1) ___ for all living beings. However, in recent years many cities have been (2) ___ from acute water shortage. The main reason is the (3) ___ use of groundwater. If we do not take steps to (4) ___ water, future generations will pay a heavy price. Every citizen should therefore (5) ___ responsibility.*

| Blank | Options | Thinking | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1) | essential / optional / harmful / unknown | Topic sentence about the value of water; positive | **essential** |
| (2) | escaping / suffering / benefiting / preferring | *___ from shortage* — fixed pair and negative sense | **suffering** |
| (3) | careful / limited / excessive / rare | It is the *reason* for shortage, so it must be over-use | **excessive** |
| (4) | waste / pollute / conserve / ignore | *take steps to ___* water to avoid a heavy price → save | **conserve** |
| (5) | make / do / give / take | collocation | **take** |

Notice that *However* in sentence 2 turns from the value of water to the problem — the connector told you the tone of blank (2) before you read the options.

## 4. Para jumbles ⭐

### 4.1 The method

| Step | Look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Opener** | A sentence that introduces a person, event or topic in full: a name, *This is the story of…*, a definition, a time setting (*Historically…*, *Louis Braille was born…*) | It must make sense with nothing before it |
| 2. **Not the opener** | Sentences starting with a pronoun (*It, He, They, This*), a connector (*However, As a result, Therefore, Yet*), or *the other / another / also* | These need an earlier sentence |
| 3. **Mandatory pairs** | pronoun → the noun it refers to; *one … the other*; problem → *however* → solution; cause → *as a result*; *It was then that* → the event it refers to | Lock pairs; most options then fall away |
| 4. **Chronology** | dates, ages, *at first → slowly → within a few years → today* | Events usually run forward in time |
| 5. **General → specific** | Topic → statistic or example → comment → conclusion | Expository paragraphs follow this |
| 6. **Closer** | Result, moral, present situation (*Today…*), solution, call to action | It ends the idea |
| 7. **Check options** | Eliminate options with the wrong first sentence, then test locked pairs | Usually 1–2 checks are enough |

### 4.2 The 2023–24 jumbles, solved ⭐

| Jumble | Opener and why | Locked pair | Closer | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Road safety | 4 *Road safety rules are meant for…* (topic) | 1 *should, however, realise…* comes after the problem (2) | 3 benefit of following rules | 4, 2, 1, 3 |
| Cataract | 2 *Cataract is the major cause of blindness* (definition) | 4 *It occurs more often in old age* → 1 lens hardens with age → 3 *It obstructs the light* | 3 | 2, 4, 1, 3 |
| MiG crash | A *Two IAF MiG fighters… crashed* | D *one plane crashed near Pensara* → C *The other fighter plane…* | B *As a result, a fire broke out* | A D C B |
| Library | D *Yesterday I was told that we had exhausted our funds* (problem) | C *That means, no books* → B *However, I have a solution* | A the solution | D C B A |
| Telephone booth | A *This is the story of…* | C people fail to open the door → B a boy sings | D *the door opened on its own* | A C B D |
| Braille | A *Louis Braille was born on 4 Jan 1809* | D lost eyesight at 3 in an accident → C *He had been playing in his father's workshop when the accident took place* | B doctors could not save his eyes | A D C B |
| Great Plains | Q *Historically, fires … burned naturally* | R Native Americans also burned → P settlers saw fire as a threat | S *In the last 50 years… fire has returned* | Q R P S |

### 4.3 Sentence-part rearrangement ⭐
The first part is given; arrange P, Q, R, S so that the sentence is grammatical.
1. Join the given part to the only option that can follow it grammatically (*Developing basic* + **Q** *infrastructure and the production sector*).
2. Find the verb of the subject (**P** *is the only way to face an*).
3. Join article to noun (*an* → **S** *economic crisis and the Centre has the*).
4. The leftover part ends the sentence (**R** *responsibility to help states*). Answer **QPSR**.

**Clues**: an article or adjective at the end of a part needs a noun at the start of the next; *to* needs a verb; *so that* needs a subject + verb; a relative pronoun (*who, which*) follows its noun.

## 5. Practice strategy for the last month
- Read **one editorial or feature article a day** (newspaper opinion page) and ask yourself: main idea, tone, one inference, three new words.
- Solve **two para jumbles a day**; write down the link word that decided each order.
- Solve **one cloze passage every two days**; collect the collocations you missed.
- In mocks, time the passage separately: target **7 minutes for 5 questions**.

## Exam traps
1. **"NOT mentioned"** — the answer may be something true in real life; the test is only whether the passage says it ⭐ (*bicycles navigate traffic jams*).
2. **Title** must cover the whole passage — *Travel and Language*, not *7 Wonders of the World* ⭐.
3. **Expository** = explains a topic (causes, effects, remedies); do not choose *argumentative* just because the topic is serious ⭐.
4. **Antonym from the passage**: the answer is a word printed in the passage; the other options are usually synonyms of the given word ⭐.
5. In **word-meaning** questions, the context meaning wins over the first dictionary meaning (*subtle* competition = *implied*, not *thin*) ⭐.
6. In para jumbles, a sentence with **It / He / The other / However / As a result** can never be first ⭐.
7. In **cloze**, *seldom, hardly, rarely* reverse the meaning of the verb that follows.
8. Do not answer RC from memory of the topic — the passage on population said *every sixth person is Indian*; answer with the passage's figures.
