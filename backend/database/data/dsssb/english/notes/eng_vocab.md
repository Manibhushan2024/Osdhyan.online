# Vocabulary: Synonyms, Antonyms, One-word Substitution, Idioms, Spelling

**In 30 seconds**
- Vocabulary is the **biggest block of DSSSB English**: 36 of the 80 questions in 2023–24 (about 9 per paper).
- **Spelling** ⭐ (8): pick the correct word, the wrong word, or the sentence with no spelling error — *horizontal, referred, indispensable, allot, university, permeate, crucial, necessary, consensus, issue*.
- **Idioms and proverbs** ⭐ (9): *blow off steam, miss the boat, short fuse, break even, face the music, call it a day, roll up one's sleeves, read between the lines, you can't have your cake and eat it too*.
- **One-word substitution** ⭐ (6): *egoist, gerontology, arsenal, anthropology, chronology, dormitory* — mostly -ology words and places.
- **Antonyms/synonyms** ⭐ (6): *propensity ↔ disinclination, predictable ↔ improbable, liberation ↔ subjugation, quarantine ↔ assimilate, friendly ↔ hostile, obstinate = headstrong*.
- **Homophones/homonyms** ⭐ (3: *heel/heal, meet/meat, waves*), **collocations** ⭐ (*fast train, fully aware*), **phrasal verbs** ⭐ (*gear up for, take to the streets*).
- Method: know the **root**, check whether the word is **positive or negative**, and remove options that are synonyms of each other — the odd one out is often the answer.

## 2. How to crack synonyms and antonyms

| Step | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Root | Break the word into prefix/root/suffix | *bene* (good) + *volent* (wishing) → benevolent = kind |
| 2. Charge | Decide if the word is positive or negative | *pernicious* sounds harmful → synonym *harmful* |
| 3. Remove twins | Two options that mean the same cannot both be wrong for a single-answer question — but both cannot be right either | *propensity*: aptness, disposition (near-synonyms) → antonym is **disinclination** ⭐ |
| 4. Context | If the word is underlined in a sentence, replace it with each option | *Garima has a propensity for being the attention seeker* |
| 5. Part of speech | Synonym and word must be the same part of speech | *alleviation* (noun) – in RC the nearest option given was *eradicate* ⭐ |

## 7. Homophones and homonyms ⭐

**Spelling rules worth knowing**: (1) *i before e except after c* — believe, achieve, but receive, ceiling (exceptions: weird, seize, foreign). (2) Double the final consonant before *-ed/-ing* when the stress is on the last syllable: *refer → referred, begin → beginning, occur → occurred* ⭐, but *visit → visited, open → opened*. (3) Keep the silent *e* before *-able* after *c/g*: *noticeable, manageable*; drop it otherwise: *lovable, usable*. (4) *-ible/-able*: indispens**able** ⭐, irresist**ible**, permiss**ible**, accept**able**. (5) Words ending in *-ful* have one *l*: *successful, careful*.

## Exam traps
1. In a spelling-sentence question, check **every long word** — DSSSB hides two or three errors per wrong option (*horrizontal / refferred / refered*) ⭐.
2. **Referred, occurred, preferred** double the *r*; **offered, visited, benefited** do not (stress rule) ⭐.
3. **Indispensable** ends in *-able*; **irresistible, permissible** end in *-ible* ⭐.
4. Antonym questions often include two or three **near-synonyms** of the word — the odd one out is the answer (*aptness, disposition* vs **disinclination**) ⭐.
5. **Gerontology** (ageing) vs **geriatrics** (medical care of the old) vs **genealogy** (family tree) ⭐.
6. **Apiary** (bees) vs **aviary** (birds) vs **arsenal** (weapons) vs **archive** (records) ⭐.
7. An idiom's meaning is **never literal**: *miss the boat* is not about boats, *face the music* is not about music ⭐.
8. **Homophone blanks** test part of speech: noun first then verb (*My injured heel will heal*) ⭐.
