# English Language: DSSSB TGT Revision Notes

## 1. One-page summary

- About 20 questions in the paper (20 marks). Roughly 8-10 are grammar (error spotting, sentence improvement, fill in the blanks, voice, narration), 6-8 are vocabulary (synonyms, antonyms, one-word, idioms, spelling) and 4-5 are comprehension or cloze.
- Most scoring order: vocabulary (fast, 10 seconds each) then grammar rules then passages.
- Always read the sentence for tense first, then subject-verb agreement, then preposition or article.
- Wrong answers cost 0.25 mark, so eliminate two options before guessing.
- In a passage, answer from the text only, never from general knowledge.

## 2. Tenses

| Tense | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present | writes | is writing | has written | has been writing |
| Past | wrote | was writing | had written | had been writing |
| Future | will write | will be writing | will have written | will have been writing |

- Habits and universal truths: simple present. Actions at this moment ("Look!", "Listen!", "now"): present continuous.
- "Since" (point of time) and "for" (period): present perfect or present perfect continuous.
- Earlier of two past actions: past perfect. "Hardly/Scarcely ... when" and "No sooner ... than" take past perfect plus inversion.
- "By + future time": future perfect.
- Stative verbs (know, like, believe, belong, own, want) are not used in continuous tenses.
- Second conditional: If + past (were), would + V1. Third conditional: If + had + V3, would have + V3.
- After a past reporting verb, a universal truth stays in the present.

## 3. Articles

- A before a consonant sound (a university, a European, a one-rupee note). An before a vowel sound (an hour, an honest man, an MBA, an X-ray).
- The: unique things (the sun), rivers, seas, mountain ranges, newspapers, musical instruments (play the guitar), superlatives, "the only", "the same", "the + adjective" for a class (the poor).
- No article: names of people, countries (except the USA, the UK), languages, meals, games, and institutions used for their purpose (go to school, go to bed, go to church).
- "What a/an + singular noun" in exclamations (What a pity!). "Such a/an", "quite a", "half an hour", "many a".

## 4. Prepositions (commonly tested)

good at, bad at, weak in, interested in, absent from, accused of, afraid of, ashamed of, beware of, proud of, jealous of, fond of, died of (disease), suffering from, prevented from, annoyed with (person), angry at/with, married to, senior/junior/superior/inferior/prefer to, congratulate on, divide among (3+), between (2), differ from, different from, convince of, insist on, depend on, listen to, wait for, laugh at, agree to (a proposal) / with (a person), look into (investigate), look after (care for), put off (postpone), call off (cancel), give up (quit), run into (meet), break down (fail).
- "On" a day/date, "in" a month/year, "at" a clock time. "Since/for" with perfect tenses.

## 5. Subject-verb agreement: 25 rules

1. Each, every, either, neither, one, everyone, anybody take a singular verb.
2. "Each of / one of / either of / neither of + plural noun" is singular.
3. Neither...nor, either...or, not only...but also: verb agrees with the nearer subject.
4. As well as, along with, together with, with, in addition to: verb agrees with the first subject.
5. Two subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb.
6. Two nouns forming one idea (bread and butter, rice and curry) take a singular verb.
7. "The number of" is singular. "A number of" is plural.
8. Many a + singular noun takes a singular verb.
9. Subjects in -ics (mathematics, physics, economics) are singular.
10. News, innings, summons, gallows, measles, mumps are singular.
11. Police, cattle, people, gentry, poultry are plural.
12. Scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans, pliers are plural. "A pair of scissors" is singular.
13. Furniture, luggage, information, advice, scenery, equipment are uncountable and singular.
14. Collective nouns (committee, jury, team) are singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately.
15. Plural amounts of money, time, distance, weight used as a unit are singular (Ten kilometres is far).
16. "One of the + plural noun + who/that" takes a plural verb (He is one of the boys who have won).
17. "The only one of ... who" takes a singular verb.
18. "The + adjective" (the rich, the poor) is plural.
19. Titles of books and films are singular (The Arabian Nights is interesting).
20. A relative pronoun takes its antecedent's number and person.
21. "More than one + singular noun" takes a singular verb.
22. "Few, many, several, both" are plural. "A little, much" take a singular verb.
23. Sentences beginning with "There/Here" agree with the noun that follows.
24. "None" can be singular or plural; "none of the + uncountable" is singular.
25. Fractions and percentages agree with the noun after "of" (Half of the cake is gone; half of the students are absent).

## 6. Error spotting and sentence improvement checklist

Look in this order: (1) tense and time words, (2) subject-verb agreement, (3) pronoun case (between you and me; he is taller than I), (4) preposition after the verb or adjective, (5) article, (6) adjective or adverb (hardly vs hard), (7) double negative or double comparative (more taller), (8) parallelism (prefers walking to driving), (9) conjunction pairs (though...yet, not both "though" and "but"; hardly...when; no sooner...than; scarcely...when).
- Do not pluralise: advice, information, furniture, luggage, news, hair, knowledge.
- "Despite" and "in spite of" never take "of" after despite.
- "Unless" already means "if not"; use no "not" after it.

## 7. Active/passive and direct/indirect

**Passive forms:** simple present is/am/are + V3; simple past was/were + V3; present continuous is being + V3; past continuous was being + V3; present perfect has/have been + V3; past perfect had been + V3; future will be + V3; future perfect will have been + V3; modals can/may/should be + V3. Imperative: Let + object + be + V3. "Who" becomes "By whom".

**Narration changes:** present to past, past to past perfect, will to would, can to could, may to might. Now to then, today to that day, tomorrow to the next day, yesterday to the previous day, here to there, this to that. Questions: asked + if/whether (yes/no) or the same wh-word, statement word order. Commands: ordered/told + to-infinitive. Requests: requested. Advice: advised. Exclamations: exclaimed with joy/sorrow. "Let us": proposed/suggested that ... should. No tense change for universal truth or when the reporting verb is present.

## 12. Frequently asked in DSSSB

- Error spotting with a sentence split into four segments (2-3 per paper): mostly subject-verb agreement, tense, preposition and article errors.
- Sentence improvement ("select the best substitute for the underlined part"), including "No substitution required".
- Idiom or proverb meaning, one-word substitution, synonym or antonym of a word in a sentence.
- Spelling: "select the sentence with no spelling error".
- Cloze test and reading comprehension with vocabulary-in-context questions.
- Active/passive and direct/indirect: nearly every paper has one of each.
- Para-jumbles (sentence ordering): find the opening sentence, then link by pronouns and connectors.

## 13. Fifteen quick-revision one-liners

1. Each, every, either, neither: always singular.
2. Hardly/scarcely take "when"; no sooner takes "than".
3. Prefer, senior, junior, superior: use "to", never "than".
4. Since = point of time, for = period.
5. "An" depends on sound, not spelling (an hour, a unit).
6. Between = two, among = more than two.
7. Advice, furniture, information, news: no plural.
8. "If I were" for imaginary conditions.
9. Universal truth: simple present, even in reported speech.
10. Passive of "Who": By whom.
11. Stative verbs have no continuous form.
12. Neither...nor, either...or: nearer subject decides the verb.
13. Despite never takes "of"; use "in spite of".
14. "One of the + plural noun who" takes a plural verb.
15. Suggest is followed by "that + subject + verb", never by "me to".

## 14. Five common traps

1. Lookalike words: the option "stationary" vs "stationery", "principal" vs "principle".
2. "No error" is a real answer. Do not hunt for an error that is not there; about one in six error-spotting items has none.
3. Plural-looking singular subjects (mathematics, news) and singular-looking plurals (police, cattle).
4. Words between the subject and verb ("The Principal, along with the teachers, is...") mislead you into choosing a plural verb.
5. In synonym and antonym questions, check the direction: students pick the synonym when asked for the antonym. Read the instruction twice.
