# Arithmetic: DSSSB TGT Revision Notes

## 1. One-page summary

- Arithmetical and Numerical Ability carries about 20 marks. Expect roughly 12-14 questions from the topics in these notes and the rest from algebra, geometry, mensuration and data interpretation.
- Highest-yield topics: percentage, profit and loss, ratio, average, time and work, time-speed-distance, simple and compound interest, ages, number system.
- Aim for 45 seconds per question. If a question needs more than 90 seconds, skip it; there is a 0.25 penalty.
- Always convert to a common base: assume 100 for percentage questions, use the LCM of days for work questions, and use 5/18 to change km/h into m/s.
- Check the answer against the options before finalising: the unit digit or divisibility often removes two choices at once.

## 2. Number system

| Rule | Test |
|---|---|
| Divisible by 3 / 9 | digit sum divisible by 3 / 9 |
| Divisible by 4 / 8 | last 2 / 3 digits divisible by 4 / 8 |
| Divisible by 11 | (sum of digits at odd places) - (sum at even places) = 0 or multiple of 11 |
| Divisible by 7, 11, 13 | split in groups of 3 from the right and subtract alternately |
| Sum of first n natural numbers | n(n + 1)/2 |
| Sum of first n odd / even numbers | n^2 / n(n + 1) |
| Sum of squares / cubes | n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 / [n(n + 1)/2]^2 |

- Unit digit cycles: 2, 3, 7, 8 repeat every 4 powers; 4 and 9 repeat every 2; 0, 1, 5, 6 never change.
- Example: unit digit of 3^47. The cycle is 3, 9, 7, 1. 47 = 4 x 11 + 3, so the third term: 7.
- Remainder trick: find a power that gives remainder 1. 2^3 = 8 leaves 1 with 7, so 2^50 = (2^3)^16 x 2^2 leaves 4.
- Example: 3685: (3 + 8) - (6 + 5) = 0, so divisible by 11 (3685 = 11 x 335).
- Number of multiples of k between a and b = (last - first)/k + 1.
- Two remainder conditions: list numbers satisfying the larger divisor first and test them. Remainder 3 with 5 and 5 with 7: 5, 12, 19, 26, 33 gives 33.

## 3. LCM and HCF

- HCF x LCM = product of two numbers (for two numbers only).
- HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators / LCM of denominators. LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators / HCF of denominators.
- Largest number dividing a, b, c leaving remainders r1, r2, r3: HCF of (a - r1), (b - r2), (c - r3).
- Smallest number leaving the same remainder r when divided by a, b, c: LCM + r.
- Smallest number divisible by a, b, c: LCM. Largest n-digit number divisible by k: largest n-digit number minus its remainder with k.
- Example: bells ringing at 12, 18, 30 minutes: LCM = 180 minutes = 3 hours.
- Example: product 1620, HCF 9, so LCM = 180.

## 4. Simplification and BODMAS

- Order: Brackets, Orders (powers and roots), Division and Multiplication (left to right), Addition and Subtraction (left to right).
- Memorise squares up to 30, cubes up to 15, and these identities: (a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2; a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b).
- Decimal products: count the total decimal places in the factors. 0.25 x 0.4 = 0.10.
- Fractions: dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal.
- Example: 7/8 of 96 - 5/6 of 72 = 84 - 60 = 24.

## 5. Percentage

| Fraction | Percent | Fraction | Percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50 | 1/7 | 14.28 |
| 1/3 | 33.33 | 1/8 | 12.5 |
| 1/4 | 25 | 1/9 | 11.11 |
| 1/5 | 20 | 1/10 | 10 |
| 1/6 | 16.67 | 1/12 | 8.33 |

- Successive changes of x% and y%: net = x + y + xy/100 (use negative signs for decreases).
- Example: +10% then +20% = 10 + 20 + 2 = 32%. A rise of 20% then fall of 20% gives 20 - 20 - 4 = -4%.
- Price rises r%: reduce consumption by r/(100 + r) x 100. For 25%, reduce by 20%.
- If A is x% more than B, then B is x/(100 + x) x 100 % less than A. For 20% more, B is 16 2/3% less.
- Election and marks problems: convert to the difference of percentages. Winner 55%, loser 45%, margin 10%.
- Population growth: P x (1 + r/100)^n.

## 6. Ratio, proportion and average

- A : B and B : C: make the common term equal. 2 : 3 and 4 : 5 becomes 8 : 12 : 15.
- Mean proportional between a and b = square root of ab. Third proportional to a, b = b^2/a. Fourth proportional to a, b, c = bc/a.
- Dividing a sum in the ratio p : q : r: each share = (part / sum of parts) x total.
- Fractional ratios: multiply by the LCM of denominators. 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/6 becomes 3 : 2 : 1.
- Average = total / number. When a member is added or removed, work with totals.
- Example: average age 15 of 40 students, 16 with the teacher. Teacher = 41 x 16 - 40 x 15 = 56.
- Replacement: new person = old person + (number of persons x change in average).
- Consecutive numbers: the average is the middle value.

## 7. Profit, loss and discount

- Profit % = (SP - CP)/CP x 100. SP = CP x (100 + p)/100. CP = SP x 100/(100 + p).
- Discount % is on the marked price; profit % is on the cost price.
- Successive discounts d1 and d2: net = d1 + d2 - d1 d2/100. 25% and 20% gives 25 + 20 - 5 = 40%.
- Marked up m% and discount d%: profit % = (100 + m)(100 - d)/100 - 100. For m = 25, d = 10: 112.5 - 100 = 12.5%.
- Two articles sold at the same price, one at x% profit and one at x% loss: always a loss of (x/10)^2 %.
- If CP of x articles equals SP of y articles: profit % = (x - y)/y x 100.
- Example: CP of 12 articles = SP of 10 articles: profit = 2/10 x 100 = 20%.

## 8. Simple and compound interest

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Simple interest | SI = PRT/100; A = P(1 + RT/100) |
| Compound interest | A = P(1 + R/100)^n; CI = A - P |
| Half-yearly | rate R/2, periods 2n |
| CI - SI (2 years) | P(R/100)^2 |
| CI - SI (3 years) | P(R/100)^2 (300 + R)/100 |
| Doubling (SI) | time = 100/R years |

- Example: 8000 at 5% for 2 years: A = 8000 x 1.1025 = 8820, so CI = 820 while SI = 800.
- If SI for 2 years at 5% is 500, then P = 500/(0.05 x 2) = 5000.
- Compare years with years: convert the rate and the period to the same unit before computing.

## 9. Time and work, pipes and cisterns

- Efficiency method: take total work = LCM of days. A in 12 days and B in 15 days: total 60 units, A does 5 per day, B does 4 per day. Together 9 per day, so 60/9 = 6 2/3 days.
- Two persons: together time = ab/(a + b). If one works x days and leaves, subtract the work done and continue with the remaining.
- Wages are divided in the ratio of work done (efficiency x days).
- Man-days: M1 D1 / W1 = M2 D2 / W2.
- Pipes: inlet is positive work, outlet (or leak) is negative work. Net rate = sum of rates.
- Example: filled by a pipe in 6 hours, leak empties it in 8 hours: net = 1/6 - 1/8 = 1/24, so 24 hours.
- Leak rate = rate without leak - rate with leak. A pipe fills in 15 hours, but 20 hours with a leak: leak = 1/15 - 1/20 = 1/60, so it empties the tank in 60 hours.

## 10. Time-speed-distance, trains and boats

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| km/h to m/s | multiply by 5/18 |
| m/s to km/h | multiply by 18/5 |
| Average speed (equal distances) | 2xy/(x + y) |
| Train crossing a pole or a man | length / speed |
| Train crossing a platform | (length + platform) / speed |
| Two trains, opposite / same direction | speeds add / subtract |
| Boat downstream / upstream | u + v / u - v |
| Still-water speed and stream | (D + U)/2 and (D - U)/2 |

- Speed changes to a/b of usual: time becomes b/a of usual. At 5/6 speed, the extra 1/5 of the usual time is the delay.
- Example: 200 m train at 72 km/h (20 m/s) crossing a 300 m platform: 500/20 = 25 s.
- Example: boat 10 km/h, stream 2 km/h, 48 km each way: 48/12 + 48/8 = 4 + 6 = 10 hours.
- Two trains of lengths l1 and l2: crossing time = (l1 + l2) / relative speed.

## 11. Mixtures, alligation and ages

- Alligation: ratio of cheaper : dearer = (dearer - mean) : (mean - cheaper).
- Example: 30 and 50 mixed to make 36: ratio = (50 - 36) : (36 - 30) = 14 : 6 = 7 : 3.
- Replacement: quantity left after n replacements = initial x (1 - x/total)^n. 60 litres, 12 drawn twice: 60 x (4/5)^2 = 38.4 litres.
- Water has cost 0 in alligation of milk and water.
- Ages: let the present age be x, convert each statement to an equation, then solve. "After t years" adds t; "t years ago" subtracts t.
- With ratios, assume ages as ax and bx and form the equation after the time change.
- Example: father 40, son 10. After x years: 40 + x = 2(10 + x), so x = 20.

## 12. Frequently asked in DSSSB

- Percentage and ratio word problems with two or three numbers (student marks, income and expenditure).
- Profit, loss and discount with marked price and successive discount.
- Average with replacement, and age problems with ratios after a number of years.
- Time and work, pipes (one inlet and one outlet), and train crossing a platform or pole.
- Number system: divisibility, unit digit, remainder; LCM and HCF word problems.
- Simple and compound interest for 2 years, and the CI - SI difference.

## 13. Fifteen quick-revision one-liners

1. Sum of first n natural numbers = n(n + 1)/2.
2. HCF x LCM = product of two numbers.
3. Divisibility by 11: odd-place sum minus even-place sum.
4. km/h x 5/18 = m/s.
5. Average speed for equal distances = 2xy/(x + y).
6. Net of x% and y% = x + y + xy/100.
7. Price up r%: cut consumption by r/(100 + r) x 100.
8. Equal selling price, equal profit and loss percentage: loss = (x/10)^2 %.
9. CI - SI (2 years) = P(R/100)^2.
10. Two persons together: ab/(a + b) days.
11. Downstream = u + v; upstream = u - v.
12. Train crossing a platform: add both lengths.
13. Alligation: cross-subtract and take the difference.
14. After n replacements: initial x (1 - x/total)^n.
15. Ages: equation from each statement, then verify with the original condition.

## 14. Five common traps

1. Taking the average speed as the simple average of two speeds when the distance is equal; use 2xy/(x + y).
2. Adding successive percentages without the xy/100 term.
3. Treating discount as a percentage of cost price instead of the marked price.
4. Mixing km/h and m/s, or minutes and hours, in the same equation.
5. In train problems, forgetting the length of the train (or using only one train's length when two trains cross).
