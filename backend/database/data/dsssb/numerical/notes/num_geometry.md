# Geometry & Mensuration

**In 30 seconds**
- 10 of the 76 numerical questions in the 2023–24 DSSSB TGT CS papers: **mensuration 9, geometry 1**. The favourites: solids melted and recast ⭐ (cones → sphere), equal volumes of cylinder and cone ⭐, a cylinder inside a sphere ⭐, a cone from its painting cost ⭐, a cylinder's TSA → a sphere ⭐, a circular ring inside a square ⭐, the circumcircle of a right triangle ⭐, a rhombus equal in area to a rectangle ⭐, % change in a cuboid's surface area ⭐ and a rectangle from its area ⭐.
- Check for a **Pythagorean triplet** first (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 7-24-25, 8-15-17, 20-21-29, 28-45-53 ⭐): a right triangle's circumradius is half the hypotenuse.
- Melting / recasting: **volume stays the same** ⭐; surface area does not.
- Cone: l² = r² + h²; curved surface πrl; volume (1/3)πr²h ⭐. Sphere: 4πr², (4/3)πr³ ⭐.
- Cylinder inside a sphere of radius R: r² + (h/2)² = R² ⭐.
- All lengths × k → areas × k², volumes × k³ (each side −12% → area × 0.88² = 0.7744) ⭐.
- Rhombus: diagonals bisect at right angles; side² = (d₁/2)² + (d₂/2)²; area = d₁d₂/2 ⭐.

## 1. Lines, angles and triangles

| Fact | Statement |
|---|---|
| Angles on a line / at a point | 180° / 360° |
| Parallel lines and a transversal | Corresponding and alternate angles equal; co-interior angles add to 180° |
| Angle sum of a triangle | 180° |
| Exterior angle | = sum of the two interior opposite angles |
| Triangle inequality | Sum of any two sides > third side |
| Pythagoras | In a right triangle, hypotenuse² = sum of squares of the other sides |
| Isosceles | Angles opposite equal sides are equal |
| Basic proportionality (Thales) | DE ∥ BC → AD/DB = AE/EC |
| Similar triangles | Ratio of areas = (ratio of sides)² = (ratio of altitudes/medians)² |
| Angle bisector theorem | Bisector of A divides BC in the ratio AB : AC |

**Worked example.** Angles in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 → 9k = 180° → largest **80°**.
**Worked example.** Exterior angle 110°, one opposite interior angle 45° → other **65°**.
**Worked example.** DE ∥ BC, AD = 3, DB = 2, AE = 4.5 → EC = 4.5 × 2/3 = **3 cm**.
**Worked example.** Similar triangles with areas 16 : 25 → altitudes **4 : 5**.
**Shortcut:** areas → take square roots for sides; sides → square for areas.

### Centres of a triangle

| Centre | Meeting point of | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| Centroid G | Medians | Divides each median 2 : 1 from the vertex |
| Circumcentre O | Perpendicular bisectors | Equidistant from vertices (R); in a right triangle it is the midpoint of the hypotenuse ⭐ |
| Incentre I | Angle bisectors | Equidistant from sides (r = Area/s) |
| Orthocentre H | Altitudes | At the right-angle vertex in a right triangle |

| Triangle | Circumradius R | Inradius r |
|---|---|---|
| Any | abc/(4 × Area) | Area/s (s = half perimeter) |
| Right (legs a, b, hypotenuse c) ⭐ | c/2 | (a + b − c)/2 |
| Equilateral side a | a/√3 | a/(2√3) (R = 2r) |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Area of the circle circumscribing a triangle with sides 28, 45, 53 cm?
28² + 45² = 784 + 2025 = 2809 = 53² → right triangle → R = 26.5 → area = **702.25π cm²**.
**Shortcut:** test a² + b² = c² first whenever three sides are given; then R = c/2 with no Heron's formula.

**Worked example.** Circumcircle of the 20-21-29 triangle: R = 14.5 → **210.25π cm²**. Inradius of 8-15-17: (8 + 15 − 17)/2 = **3 cm**.

**Worked example.** Median to the hypotenuse of a 6-8-10 triangle = **5 cm** (half the hypotenuse).

## 2. Polygons

| Fact | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sum of interior angles | (n − 2) × 180° |
| Each interior angle (regular) | (n − 2) × 180°/n |
| Sum of exterior angles | 360° (any polygon) |
| Each exterior angle (regular) | 360°/n |
| Number of diagonals | n(n − 3)/2 |

**Worked example.** Hexagon: interior sum (6 − 2) × 180° = **720°**; each angle 120°; diagonals 9.
**Worked example.** Each exterior angle 24° → n = 360/24 = **15 sides**.
**Shortcut:** interior + exterior = 180°, so from an interior angle of 156° the exterior is 24° → 15 sides.

## 3. Circles

| Fact | Statement |
|---|---|
| Perpendicular from centre to a chord | Bisects the chord: d² + (chord/2)² = r² |
| Equal chords | Equidistant from the centre |
| Angle at the centre | Twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc |
| Angle in a semicircle | 90° |
| Angles in the same segment | Equal |
| Cyclic quadrilateral | Opposite angles add to 180° |
| Tangent ⟂ radius | Tangent length from P = √(OP² − r²) |
| Two tangents from a point | Equal in length |
| Intersecting chords | AP × PB = CP × PD |
| Tangent–secant | PT² = PA × PB |

**Worked example.** Radius 13, chord 24 → distance from centre = √(169 − 144) = **5 cm**.
**Worked example.** Point 25 cm from the centre, radius 7 → tangent = √(625 − 49) = **24 cm**.
**Worked example.** Chords meet inside: AP = 6, PB = 4, CP = 3 → PD = 24/3 = **8 cm**.
**Worked example.** Arc subtends 120° at the centre → **60°** on the major arc.
**Worked example.** Cyclic ABCD with ∠A = 70° → ∠C = **110°**.
**Shortcut:** "cyclic" in a question = opposite angles supplementary; "semicircle" = right angle.

## 4. Plane figures (2D mensuration)

| Figure | Area | Perimeter / other |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle l × b ⭐ | lb | 2(l + b); diagonal √(l² + b²) |
| Square side a | a² = d²/2 | 4a; diagonal a√2 |
| Triangle | ½ × base × height | Heron: √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)] |
| Equilateral side a | (√3/4)a² | height (√3/2)a |
| Right triangle | ½ × product of legs | |
| Parallelogram | base × height | |
| Rhombus ⭐ | ½ d₁d₂ | side = √[(d₁/2)² + (d₂/2)²]; perimeter 4 × side |
| Trapezium | ½ (sum of parallel sides) × height | |
| Circle radius r | πr² | 2πr |
| Semicircle | πr²/2 | πr + 2r |
| Sector, angle θ | (θ/360) πr² | arc = (θ/360) 2πr |
| Ring (radii R, r) ⭐ | π(R² − r²) = π(R + r)(R − r) | |
| Path of width w outside l × b | (l + 2w)(b + 2w) − lb | |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** Breadth is 5/8 of the length; floor area 160 m². Length − breadth?
L × 5L/8 = 160 → L² = 256 → L = 16, B = 10 → **6 m**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Rhombus perimeter 116 m, one diagonal 42 m; its area equals a rectangle with sides 30 : 7. Rectangle's perimeter?
Side 29; half-diagonals 21 and √(841 − 441) = 20 → diagonals 42, 40 → area 840. 30k × 7k = 210k² = 840 → k = 2 → 60 × 14 → perimeter **148 m**.
**Shortcut:** 20-21-29 is a triplet — the other half-diagonal is immediate.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A circle is inscribed in a square; a concentric circle lies inside it. Ring area 1540 cm², difference of radii 7 cm. Perimeter of the square?
R² − r² = 1540 × 7/22 = 490 → R + r = 70 → R = 38.5 → side = 77 → perimeter **308 cm**.
**Shortcut:** ring area = π × (R + r) × (R − r), so R + r = area ÷ (π × difference).

**Worked example.** Ring area 286 cm², difference 7 cm (π = 22/7) → R² − r² = 91 → R + r = 13 → **R = 10 cm**.

**Worked example.** Heron: 13, 14, 15 → s = 21 → √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = **84 cm²**.
**Worked example.** Circumference 44 cm → r = 7 → area **154 cm²**.
**Worked example.** Park 60 × 40 with a 5 m path outside → 70 × 50 − 2400 = **1100 m²**.
**Worked example.** Rhombus diagonals 16 and 12 → side 10 → perimeter **40 cm**, area 96 cm².

## 5. Solids (3D mensuration) ⭐

| Solid | Curved / lateral surface | Total surface | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cuboid l × b × h ⭐ | 2h(l + b) | 2(lb + bh + hl) | lbh; diagonal √(l² + b² + h²) |
| Cube a | 4a² | 6a² | a³; diagonal a√3 |
| Cylinder r, h ⭐ | 2πrh | 2πr(r + h) | πr²h |
| Cone r, h, l ⭐ | πrl (l² = r² + h²) | πr(l + r) | (1/3)πr²h |
| Sphere r ⭐ | 4πr² | 4πr² | (4/3)πr³ |
| Hemisphere r | 2πr² | 3πr² | (2/3)πr³ |
| Hollow cylinder (R, r, h) | 2π(R + r)h (inner + outer) | + 2π(R² − r²) | π(R² − r²)h |
| Frustum (R, r, h, slant l) | π(R + r)l | + π(R² + r²) | (1/3)πh(R² + r² + Rr) |

Ratios for the same r and h: cone : hemisphere : cylinder volumes = 1 : 2 : 3 when h = r.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Two cones of radius 21 cm and heights 31 and 53 cm are melted into a sphere. Surface area of the sphere?
Volume = (1/3)π × 441 × 84 = 12348π = (4/3)πR³ → R³ = 9261 → R = 21 → 4π × 441 = **1764π cm²**.
**Shortcut:** cones with equal radii combine: just add the heights.

**Worked example.** Cones of radius 6 cm and heights 10 and 14 cm → (1/3)π × 36 × 24 = 288π → R³ = 216 → R = 6 → area **144π cm²**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A cylinder (h 32 cm, diameter 36 cm) has the same volume as a cone of height 24 cm. Curved surface of the cone?
π × 324 × 32 = (1/3)πr² × 24 → r² = 1296 → r = 36. l = √(1296 + 576) = √1872 = 12√13 → πrl = **432√13π cm²**.
**Shortcut:** equal volumes → r_cone² = 3 × r_cyl² × h_cyl/h_cone = 3 × 324 × 32/24 = 1296.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Cylinder height 1.2R inscribed in a sphere of radius 10. Cylinder volume as % of the sphere?
h = 12, half-height 6 → r = 8 → 768π vs 4000π/3 → 768 × 3/4000 × 100 = **57.6%**.

**Worked example.** Cylinder of height 10 in a sphere of radius 13 → r = √(169 − 25) = 12 → volume **1440π cm³**.
**Shortcut:** look for a triplet with the half-height: 5-12-13, 6-8-10.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Colouring a conical tomb at ₹80 per 100 m² costs ₹440; slant height 25 m. Volume (π = 22/7)?
CSA = 440 × 100/80 = 550 → (22/7) × r × 25 = 550 → r = 7 → h = 24 → (1/3) × (22/7) × 49 × 24 = **1232 m³**.

**Worked example.** Painting at ₹3 per m² costs ₹6,600; slant 50 m → CSA 2200 → r = 14 → h = 48 → **9856 m³**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A cylinder of height 32 cm has TSA 370π; its volume is 3/5 of a sphere's. Diameter of the sphere?
2πr(r + 32) = 370π → r(r + 32) = 185 → r = 5 → volume 800π = (3/5)(4/3)πR³ = (4/5)πR³ → R³ = 1000 → diameter **20 cm**.
**Shortcut:** factor 185 = 5 × 37 and 37 − 5 = 32 ✓ — the radius pops out.

**Worked example.** Sphere radius 6 → cones r 3, h 4: 288π ÷ 12π = **24 cones**. Cuboid 12 × 8 × 6 → 2 cm cubes: 576/8 = **72 cubes**.
**Shortcut:** number of pieces = big volume ÷ small volume; cancel π first.

**Worked example.** Cube of volume 343 → a = 7 → TSA **294 cm²**. Cylinder r 7, h 10 → **1540 cm³**. Cone r 6, h 8 → l = 10 → CSA **60π cm²**. Hemisphere r 21 → TSA 3πr² = **4158 cm²**.

## 6. Percentage change in area and volume ⭐

| Change | Area (k²) | Volume (k³) |
|---|---|---|
| Each length +10% | +21% | +33.1% |
| Each length +20% | +44% | +72.8% |
| Each length −10% | −19% | −27.1% |
| Each length −12% ⭐ | −22.56% | −31.85% |
| Each length doubled | × 4 (+300%) | × 8 (+700%) |
| Radius +r%, height same (cylinder volume) | — | (1 + r/100)² |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** Each of l, b, h of a cuboid decreased by 12%. Decrease in total surface area?
0.88² = 0.7744 → **22.56%**.
**Shortcut:** for two equal changes x: 2x + x²/100 with sign → −12 − 12 + 1.44 = −22.56.

**Worked example.** Radius of a circle +10% → area +21%. Each edge of a cube +20% → surface +44%, volume +72.8%.

## 7. Special cases DSSSB-style papers like

| Situation | Key relation |
|---|---|
| Solid dropped into a cylinder of water | Rise in level × πR² = volume of the solid |
| Wire bent from one shape into another | Perimeter stays the same |
| Sector of radius R and angle θ rolled into a cone | Slant height = R; arc length (θ/360) × 2πR = 2πr of the cone |
| Toy: cone on a hemisphere (same radius) | Volume = (1/3)πr²h + (2/3)πr³; surface = πrl + 2πr² |
| Cylinder with hemispherical ends | Volume = πr²h + (4/3)πr³ |
| Largest sphere in a cube of side a | Radius a/2 |
| Largest cube in a sphere of radius r | Diagonal a√3 = 2r |
| Largest cone in a cube of side a | r = a/2, h = a |
| Square inscribed in a circle of radius r | Diagonal = 2r, area = 2r² |

**Worked example.** A sphere of radius 3 cm is dropped into a cylinder of radius 6 cm partly filled with water. Rise in level?
(4/3)π × 27 = π × 36 × h → 36π = 36πh → **h = 1 cm**.
**Shortcut:** cancel π at once; rise = (4/3)r³/R² = 36/36.

**Worked example.** A wire bent into a square of side 22 cm is re-bent into a circle. Radius (π = 22/7)?
Perimeter 88 = 2πr → r = 88 × 7/44 = **14 cm**; area 616 cm² (more than the square's 484 cm² — a circle encloses the most area for a given perimeter).

**Worked example.** A sector of radius 21 cm and angle 120° is folded into a cone. Base radius?
Arc = (120/360) × 2π × 21 = 14π = 2πr → **r = 7 cm**; slant height 21 cm, height √(441 − 49) = √392 = 14√2 cm.
**Shortcut:** r = R × θ/360 = 21 × 1/3 = 7.

**Worked example.** Largest sphere carved from a cube of side 14 cm: r = 7 → volume (4/3) × (22/7) × 343 = **1437⅓ cm³**.

**Worked example.** Equilateral triangle of side 12 cm: area (√3/4) × 144 = 36√3 cm²; inradius 12/(2√3) = 2√3 cm; circumradius 12/√3 = 4√3 cm (twice the inradius).

## Exam traps
1. Melting keeps the **volume**; do not equate surface areas ⭐.
2. Cone curved surface uses the **slant height** l, not h ⭐.
3. Total surface of a hemisphere is 3πr² (includes the flat base); curved is 2πr².
4. "Diameter" in the question → halve it before using r ⭐.
5. A circle inscribed in a square has diameter = side; a square inscribed in a circle has diagonal = diameter ⭐.
6. Surface area scales as k², volume as k³ — −12% each side is −22.56% in area, not −36% ⭐.
7. For a path outside a rectangle add 2w to each dimension, not w.
8. Rhombus area is d₁d₂/2; its side comes from **half**-diagonals ⭐.
