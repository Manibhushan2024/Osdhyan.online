# Percentage, Ratio, Average, Ages, Profit–Loss, SI–CI

**In 30 seconds**
- The biggest chapter: **35 of the 76** numerical questions in the 2023–24 DSSSB TGT CS papers came from here — percentage ⭐ (7), profit & loss ⭐ (5), simple interest ⭐ (5), discounts ⭐ (3), compound interest ⭐ (3), partnership ⭐ (3), variation ⭐ (3), ages ⭐ (2), ratio ⭐ (2), average ⭐ (1), CI − SI ⭐ (1).
- Work with **multiplying factors**: +20% → × 1.2, −25% → × 0.75; successive changes multiply ⭐.
- Successive discounts with an unknown x% ⭐: MP × (1 − x/100) × (other factors) = SP → find x, then use 2x% or 3x% as one discount.
- Partnership ⭐: profit ratio = Σ (capital × months); convert a fractional capital ratio to whole numbers first.
- Variation ⭐: write A = k × B/C, find k from the first set, then use it.
- SI: amounts for two periods → yearly interest = difference ÷ gap in years ⭐. CI: A = P(1 + r/100)^n; 2-year CI − SI = P(r/100)² ⭐.
- Equal instalments ⭐: loan = X/(1 + r) + X/(1 + r)² (annual CI); half-yearly → use r/2 per period.
- Ages: the **difference** of two ages never changes ⭐.

## 1. Percentage ⭐

### Fraction ↔ percent (learn these — they turn % work into fraction work)

| Fraction | % | Fraction | % | Fraction | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50 | 1/7 | 14 2/7 (14.28) | 1/12 | 8 1/3 |
| 1/3 | 33 1/3 | 1/8 | 12.5 | 1/15 | 6 2/3 |
| 1/4 | 25 | 1/9 | 11 1/9 (11.11) | 1/16 | 6.25 |
| 1/5 | 20 | 1/10 | 10 | 1/20 | 5 |
| 1/6 | 16 2/3 | 1/11 | 9 1/11 (9.09) | 1/25 | 4 |

### Formulas

| Situation | Formula |
|---|---|
| x% of N | x × N/100 |
| Percentage change | (New − Old)/Old × 100 |
| A is r% more than B → B is less than A by | r/(100 + r) × 100 % ⭐ |
| A is r% less than B → B is more than A by | r/(100 − r) × 100 % |
| Successive changes a% and b% | a + b + ab/100 (use − for a decrease) ⭐ |
| Price up r%, keep spending fixed → cut consumption by | r/(100 + r) × 100 % |
| Population / value after n years at r% | P(1 + r/100)^n ⭐; depreciation P(1 − r/100)^n |
| "n times more than" | means (n + 1) times as much ⭐ |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A has ₹12,528, which is 16% more than B and 20% less than C. B + C = ?
B = 12528 ÷ 1.16 = 10,800; C = 12528 ÷ 0.8 = 15,660; B + C = **₹26,460**.
**Shortcut:** "16% more than B" → divide by 1.16; "20% less than C" → divide by 0.8. Never subtract 16% from A.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Price rises successively by 20%, 10%, 5% and then falls by 40%; final price ₹415.80. Initial price?
Factor = 1.2 × 1.1 × 1.05 × 0.6 = 0.8316 → 415.80 ÷ 0.8316 = **₹500**.
**Shortcut:** multiply factors two at a time (1.2 × 0.6 = 0.72; 1.1 × 1.05 = 1.155; 0.72 × 1.155 = 0.8316).

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** E1 is 8 times more than E2. E2 is what % less than E1?
E1 = E2 + 8E2 = 9E2 → (9 − 1)/9 × 100 = **88.88%**.
**Shortcut:** "n times more" = (n + 1) times; answer = n/(n + 1) × 100.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A test has 150 questions. A candidate gets 60% of the first 90 right. What % of the rest must he get right to score 64% overall?
Needed 0.64 × 150 = 96; has 0.6 × 90 = 54; needs 42 of 60 = **70%**.
**Shortcut:** convert every percentage into a count of questions first.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** Population 30,000 grows 20% a year. After 3 years: 30000 × 1.2³ = 30000 × 1.728 = **51,840**.
**Shortcut:** 1.1² = 1.21, 1.1³ = 1.331, 1.2² = 1.44, 1.2³ = 1.728 — memorise.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A is 60% more than B; C is 55% more than (A − B). A falls by 40% and C rises by 33 1/3%. C is what % more than A?
B = 100 → A = 160, A − B = 60, C = 93. New A = 96, new C = 124 → (124 − 96)/96 × 100 = **29.2%**.
**Shortcut:** in multi-person % questions, put the base person = 100.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A's income is 32% less than B's; the average of A and B is 20% less than C. A's income rises 25% and B's falls 10%. C is what % less than the new A + B?
B = 100, A = 68, average 84 = 0.8C → C = 105. New A + B = 85 + 90 = 175 → (175 − 105)/175 × 100 = **40%**.

**Worked example.** Sugar price rises 25%. Cut in consumption to keep spending the same = 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.
**Shortcut:** r% rise → cut r/(100 + r); 1/4 rise → cut 1/5.

## 2. Ratio, proportion, variation and partnership

| Term | Formula |
|---|---|
| a : b = c : d (proportion) | ad = bc |
| Fourth proportional to a, b, c | bc/a |
| Third proportional to a, b | b²/a |
| Mean proportional of a, b | √(ab) |
| Compounding a : b and b : c | Make the common term equal (LCM) |
| Divide N in a : b : c | Share = N × part/(a + b + c) |
| Fractional ratio 1/a : 1/b : 1/c | Multiply each by the LCM of a, b, c |
| Direct variation ⭐ | A = kB |
| Inverse variation ⭐ | A = k/B |
| Joint variation ⭐ | A = kB/C or A = k/(BC) |
| Partnership ⭐ | Profit share ∝ capital × time |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** a + b + c = 3960, a : (b + c) = 3 : 8 and c : (a + b) = 4 : 5. Find b.
a = 3/11 × 3960 = 1080; c = 4/9 × 3960 = 1760; b = 3960 − 2840 = **1120**.
**Shortcut:** "x : (rest)" means x is (x-part)/(total parts) of the whole.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** P : Q : R = 6 : 5 : 3, and R is 32 less than P + Q. Find Q.
(6 + 5 − 3)k = 8k = 32 → k = 4 → Q = **20**.

**Worked example.** A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5 → A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.
**Shortcut:** multiply across: A : B : C = (2 × 4) : (3 × 4) : (3 × 5).

**Worked example.** Mean proportional of 4 and 36 = √144 = 12; third proportional to 4 and 12 = 144/4 = 36; fourth proportional to 3, 8, 15 = 120/3 = 40.

### Variation ⭐

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A ∝ B and A ∝ 1/C. B = 25, C = 18 → A = 20. Find A for B = 35, C = 16.
k = 20 × 18/25 = 14.4 → A = 14.4 × 35/16 = **31.5**.
**Shortcut:** A₂ = A₁ × (B₂/B₁) × (C₁/C₂) = 20 × 35/25 × 18/16 = 31.5 — no need to find k.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** P ∝ Q/R; Q = 2.7, R = 1.6 → P = 4.5. Find R when P = 6, Q = 15.
k = 4.5 × 1.6/2.7 = 8/3 → 6 = (8/3)(15/R) → R = **20/3**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A ∝ 1/(BC); B = 9, C = 5 → A = 12. For B = 15, C = 4: A = 12 × 45/60 = **9**.

**Worked example.** x ∝ y²/z; y = 4, z = 8 → x = 10. For y = 6, z = 12: x = 10 × (36/16) × (8/12) = **15**.

### Partnership ⭐

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A and B invest in the ratio 2/3 : 3/8. After 4 months A halves his capital and B raises his by one-third. B's share of ₹39 lakh after a year?
2/3 : 3/8 = 16 : 9 (× 24). A: 16 × 4 + 8 × 8 = 128. B: 9 × 4 + 12 × 8 = 132. Ratio 32 : 33 → B = 39 × 33/65 = **₹19.8 lakh**.
**Shortcut:** convert the fractional ratio with the LCM of the denominators before applying the % change.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Capitals 5/8 : 3/5 = 25 : 24. After 6 months A cuts 20%, B adds 50%. A's share = ₹15 lakh. Total?
A: 25 × 6 + 20 × 6 = 270; B: 24 × 6 + 36 × 6 = 360 → 3 : 4 → total = 15 × 7/3 = **₹35 lakh**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Ravi : Gaurav = 1/2 : 5/6 = 3 : 5. After 4 months Ravi doubles, Gaurav cuts 40%. Gaurav's share ₹1,32,000. Ravi's?
Ravi 3 × 4 + 6 × 8 = 60; Gaurav 5 × 4 + 3 × 8 = 44 → 15 : 11 → Ravi = 132000 × 15/11 = **₹1,80,000**.

## 3. Average ⭐

| Situation | Formula |
|---|---|
| Average | Sum ÷ number of items |
| Consecutive numbers / AP | Average = middle term = (first + last)/2 |
| Wrong entry corrected ⭐ | New average = old average + (correct − wrong)/n |
| New member joins, average rises by d | New member = old average + (n + 1)d |
| One member replaced, average rises by d | New = replaced + n × d |
| Combined groups | (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂)/(n₁ + n₂) |
| Overlapping groups (middle item counted twice) | Middle = sum of both group totals − overall total |
| Average speed (equal distances) | 2xy/(x + y) |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** Average age of 40 students is 21; one age was taken as 20 instead of 30. Correct average = 21 + 10/40 = **21.25 years**.
**Shortcut:** only the error moves the average: change = error ÷ n.

**Worked example.** Average of 40 students is 15; with the teacher it is 16. Teacher = 15 + 41 × 1 = **56 years**.
**Shortcut:** the teacher pays the old average and gives 1 year to all 41 people.

**Worked example.** Average weight of 8 people rises 2.5 kg when a 65 kg person is replaced → new person = 65 + 8 × 2.5 = **85 kg**.

**Worked example.** Average of 11 results is 50; first six 49, last six 52. Sixth result = 294 + 312 − 550 = **56**.

**Worked example.** A batsman scores 61 in his 12th innings, raising his average by 3. Old average x: 11x + 61 = 12(x + 3) → x = 25 → new average **28**.
**Shortcut:** new average = runs scored − (old innings × rise) = 61 − 11 × 3 = 28.

## 4. Problems on ages ⭐

| Fact | Use |
|---|---|
| Difference of ages is constant | Ratio-part difference = actual year difference ⭐ |
| "n years ago" / "after n years" | Subtract / add n to **every** person |
| Ratio a : b now, c : d after t years | Solve (ak + t)/(bk + t) = c/d |
| Average age of a group after t years | Rises by t |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** Amit : Ankit = 10 : 13 now and Amit is 18 years younger. Ratio after 12 years?
3 parts = 18 → 1 part = 6 → ages 60 and 78 → after 12 years 72 : 90 = **4 : 5**.
**Shortcut:** the difference of ratio terms equals the age gap.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** 6 years ago Renu : Raman = 5 : 11; 6 years from now 11 : 17. Sum of present ages?
(5x + 12)/(11x + 12) = 11/17 → 85x + 204 = 121x + 132 → x = 2. Ages then 10 and 22, now 16 and 28 → sum **44**.
**Shortcut:** both ratios have a gap of 6 parts (11 − 5 = 17 − 11), so one part means the same number of years in both; Renu moves from 5 parts to 11 parts in 12 years → 6 parts = 12 years → 1 part = 2.

**Worked example.** Father is 3 times his son; after 12 years twice. 3S + 12 = 2(S + 12) → S = **12**, father 36.

**Worked example.** Mother + daughter = 56; 4 years ago mother was 7 times daughter. M − 4 = 7(D − 4) and M = 56 − D → 8D = 80 → D = 10, M = **46**.

## 5. Profit, loss and discount ⭐

| Term | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit % | (SP − CP)/CP × 100 (always on CP) |
| SP from profit / loss | SP = CP × (100 + P)/100; SP = CP × (100 − L)/100 |
| Discount | On marked price (MP): SP = MP × (100 − d)/100 |
| Mark-up m%, discount d% ⭐ | Profit % = m − d − md/100 |
| MP/CP | (100 + P%)/(100 − d%) |
| Successive discounts d₁, d₂ ⭐ | Single equivalent = d₁ + d₂ − d₁d₂/100 |
| Same SP, one r% profit and one r% loss | Overall loss = r²/100 % |
| CP of x articles = SP of y articles | Profit % = (x − y)/y × 100 |
| False weight w instead of 1000 g | Gain % = (1000 − w)/w × 100 |
| Change of SP from loss l% to gain g% | SP₂ = SP₁ × (100 + g)/(100 − l) ⭐ |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Marked 60% above CP, then discounts of 25% and 5%. Profit?
CP 100 → MP 160 → 160 × 0.75 × 0.95 = 114 → **14%**.
**Shortcut:** CP = 100 always; multiply factors.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Selling at 11/25 of the SP gives a 34% loss. Profit when sold at 80% of SP?
(11/25)SP = 0.66 CP → SP = 1.5 CP → 0.8 × 1.5 = 1.2 → **20% profit**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** 46 articles sold for ₹2,070 at 10% loss. For 20% profit sell at: CP = 2070/0.9 = 2300 → 2300 × 1.2 = **₹2,760**.
**Shortcut:** 2070 × 120/90 = 2760 in one line.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Sold for ₹374 at a 6.5% loss. For a 9.5% gain: 374 × 109.5/93.5 = **₹438**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** MP ₹2,500 sold for ₹1,683 after x%, 10%, 12%. Price with one discount of 3x%?
2500 × 0.9 × 0.88 = 1980 → (1 − x/100) = 1683/1980 = 0.85 → x = 15 → 45% off → 2500 × 0.55 = **₹1,375**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** MP ₹600, discounts 16%, 20%, x% → ₹342.72. One discount of 2x%?
600 × 0.84 × 0.8 = 403.20 → 342.72/403.20 = 0.85 → x = 15 → 30% → **₹420**.
**Shortcut:** in both PYQs the hidden discount was 15% — check 0.85 first.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** After 25% discount a cycle sells for ₹7,200. MP = 7200 ÷ 0.75 = **₹9,600**. CP ₹230 at 12% profit → profit **₹27.60**.

**Worked example.** Two articles each sold for ₹1,200, one at 20% profit, one at 20% loss → loss = 20²/100 = **4%**.

**Worked example.** CP of 12 = SP of 10 → profit (12 − 10)/10 × 100 = **20%**.

**Worked example.** Loss 10%; had he sold for ₹150 more he would gain 5% → 15% of CP = 150 → CP = **₹1,000**.

## 6. Simple interest ⭐

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| SI | P × R × T/100 |
| Amount | P(1 + RT/100) |
| Two amounts A₁ (T₁ years), A₂ (T₂ years) ⭐ | Yearly SI = (A₂ − A₁)/(T₂ − T₁); P = A₁ − T₁ × yearly SI |
| Sum becomes n times in T years | R = (n − 1) × 100/T |
| Different rates for different periods ⭐ | SI = P(R₁T₁ + R₂T₂ + …)/100 |
| Months | T = months/12 |
| Equal instalments under SI ⭐ | Equate amounts at the last date: loan grows for full time, each instalment grows for the time left |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** A sum amounts to ₹6,747 in 3½ years and ₹7,410 in 5 years. Rate?
SI for 1½ years = 663 → per year 442. P = 6747 − 3.5 × 442 = 5200. R = 442/5200 × 100 = **8.5%**.
**Shortcut:** P = (A₁T₂ − A₂T₁)/(T₂ − T₁) = (6747 × 5 − 7410 × 3.5)/1.5 = 5200.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** At 10% SI a sum amounts to ₹9,417 in 4 years 4 months. Amount at 12½% for 2 2/5 years?
P × (1 + 13/30) = 9417 → P = 9417 × 30/43 = 6570. SI = 6570 × 0.125 × 2.4 = 1971 → **₹8,541**.
**Shortcut:** 12½% × 2.4 years = 30% exactly, so amount = 1.3 × 6570.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Amount at 7.4% for 2½ years is ₹1,701 less than the SI at 15% for 10 years. Sum?
1.5P − 1.185P = 0.315P = 1701 → P = **₹5,400**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** SI for 11 years is ₹42,000; 3% for 4 years, 9% for 7 years. 12% + 63% = 75% of P = 42000 → P = **₹56,000**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Item ₹1,600 cash, or ₹700 down + 3 monthly instalments of ₹310. SI rate?
Balance 900. 900 + 900 × r × 3/1200 = 930 + 310 × r × (2 + 1 + 0)/1200 → 2700r − 930r = 36000 → r = **20.3%**.
**Shortcut:** instalment interest months add up as 2 + 1 + 0 = 3 for three monthly instalments.

**Worked example.** At what SI rate does a sum double in 8 years? R = 100/8 = **12.5%**.

## 7. Compound interest ⭐

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Amount (annual) | A = P(1 + R/100)^n |
| CI | A − P |
| Half-yearly | Rate R/2, periods 2n |
| Quarterly | Rate R/4, periods 4n |
| Different rates each year | P(1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100)… |
| CI − SI, 2 years ⭐ | P(R/100)² |
| CI − SI, 3 years | P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) |
| SI and CI for 2 years given ⭐ | R = 2(CI − SI)/SI × 100; P = SI/2 × 100/R |
| Two equal annual instalments ⭐ | Loan = X/(1 + r) + X/(1 + r)² |
| Effective rate for 2 years | 2R + R²/100 (10% → 21%; 20% → 44%) |

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** CI on ₹14,400 at 10% for 3 years: 14400 × 1.331 = 19166.40 → CI = **₹4,766.40**.
**Shortcut:** 3-year effective rate at 10% = 33.1% → 0.331 × 14400.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, Feb 2024).** SI and CI for 2 years are ₹28,800 and ₹36,000. Principal?
Yearly SI = 14400; CI − SI = 7200 = interest on 14400 for 1 year → R = 50% → P = 14400/0.5 = **₹28,800**.

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** ₹13,500 repaid in two equal annual instalments at 16% CI. Total interest?
X(1/1.16 + 1/1.3456) = 13500 → X × 2.16/1.3456 = 13500 → X = 8410 → paid 16820 → interest **₹3,320**.
**Shortcut:** with r as a fraction (16% = 4/25), X = P × (1 + r)²/(2 + r).

**Worked example ⭐ (DSSSB, June 2023).** Computer ₹75,300 cash or ₹25,740 down + two equal half-yearly instalments at 20% p.a. compounded half-yearly. Interest paid?
Balance 49560; rate 10% per half-year → X = 49560 × 1.21/2.1 = 28556 → total 57112 → interest **₹7,552**.

**Worked example.** ₹12,000 for 1½ years at 10% compounded half-yearly: 12000 × 1.05³ = 13891.50 → CI **₹1,891.50**.

**Worked example.** CI − SI on ₹5,000 at 10% for 2 years = 5000 × 0.01 = **₹50**.

**Worked example.** SI for 2 years at 5% is ₹500 → P = 5000 → CI = 5000 × 0.1025 = **₹512.50**.
**Shortcut:** CI (2 years) = SI × (1 + R/200) = 500 × 1.025.

## Exam traps
1. "x% more than B" → divide by (1 + x/100); do not take x% of A ⭐.
2. "8 times more than" means 9 times as much ⭐.
3. Successive discounts multiply; 20% + 10% is 28%, not 30% ⭐.
4. Discount is on the marked price, profit on the cost price ⭐.
5. In partnership, convert a fractional capital ratio to integers **before** the % change, and multiply by months ⭐.
6. In SI instalments the last instalment earns no interest (0 months) ⭐.
7. CI half-yearly: halve the rate **and** double the periods ⭐.
8. Ages: "n years ago" applies to both persons; the age gap never changes ⭐.
